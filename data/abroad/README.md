# Abroad inscriptions and names: the two lists

**List 1, `abroad_inscriptions.csv`** (built by `scripts/build_abroad_list.py`): every Indus inscription found outside the Indus region in the project's sources (export, held-out-from-export set, Laursen 2010 Fig. 11, extra_texts). 42 texts after removing duplicates. Columns mark where a "man" sign (G90 and its compounds, G91 twin men) sits and which signs follow it.

Descriptive comparison (abroad texts already seen, so not a test):

| | Home (exploration, 1,523 texts) | Abroad (42) |
|---|---|---|
| Contain a "man" sign | 8.6% | 52% |
| Start with a "man" sign | 1.9% | 33% |

- `G415 G803 G1` opens two Gulf seals (Ur; "Dilmun"). `G415 G803` never occurs together in the home texts.
- G91 (twin men) is the whole text on the Kalba seal and on Laursen seal 6 (Qal'at al-Bahrain).
- G55 (twelve strokes) occurs on 5 Gulf seals.

**List 2, `cuneiform_names.csv`**: personal names of Meluhhan and Dilmun people attested in cuneiform. It is short: only two names (Nanaza, Samar) are plausibly in the Meluhhan language and of the right date (c. 2030 BC); the Dilmun royal names (Ri'mum, Yagli-El, Sumu-lel) are Amorite and some 300 years later than the Gulf Type seals.

**What can and cannot be done with them.** Two names are too few for any test. What the lists support: the Gulf texts after a "man" sign are short (mostly 1–3 signs), the length expected for a short name written in syllables or a name of one or two word-signs. Nanaza has a repeated first syllable (na-na); if Gulf scribes spelled such names by sound, a doubled sign would be expected sometimes, but no abroad text has a sign repeated next to itself after a man sign. A stronger list needs the Dilmun-trade texts from Ur (Old Babylonian) and the Ur III texts naming Dilmun and Meluhha people, which list travellers and traders by name.

## Seal-legend formula: "NAME, son of NAME" (added 26 October 2026)

A round seal with an Indus bull in Paris carries a cuneiform legend read (preliminarily, Glassner 2002) as **Ur-Ninildum dumu Ur-gi**: "Ur-Ninildum, son of Ur-gi". Mesopotamian owner legends follow this pattern. Vidale (2004) suggests the Indus "man" sign may have marked descent ("son of") on western seals.

Where the man sign sits in the 42 abroad texts (descriptive): alone 3, first 11, **between two sign groups 7**, last 1. The seven: Bahrain `G160 | G90 | G55 G60 G190`; Saar `G55 G220 | G91 | G1 G93 G31`; Janabiyah `… G829 G55 | G125 | G90 G55`; Susa `G365 | G90 | G407 G604 G700`; Ur `G350 | G90 | G4`; Gonur `G285 G2 G235 G220 | G125 | G590 G390 G740`; unprovenanced `? | G91 | G603 G604`.

So abroad texts show both shapes a name formula would produce: "MAN + name" (like the cuneiform personal determinative) and "name + MAN + name" (like "X son of Y"). G55 (twelve strokes) appears right next to the man sign on three Bahrain-area seals.
