# Rakhigarhi graffiti in Nath, *Excavations at Rakhigarhi 1997–98 to 1999–2000* (ASI draft, 2014): what is and is not there

Read 30 Oct 2026 from the full PDF supplied by Erin (396 pp.). The source is rarebooksocietyofindia.org.

- **Chapter 8, "Graffiti" (pp. 205–221).** The report states: "Drawing of graffiti marks remains to be prepared for insertion".
  - The catalogue of **163 graffiti sherds** (RGR-1; pp. 208–217) has an empty "Graffiti" column. **The marks themselves are not drawn anywhere in the draft.**
  - The catalogue does give, for every sherd: locus or trench, layer, depth in cm, vessel type (some), pre- or post-firing, incision depth, and ware.
  - So **context exists without the signs.**
- **Report's own observations** (p. 206 ff.):
  - 44% of Early Harappan and 70% of Mature Harappan graffiti resemble script signs.
  - Early Harappan marks are mostly 1–14 strokes.
  - Rim and base marks are mostly made before firing and are potter's marks.
  - Belly and neck marks are made after firing and look like script, "may be the owner's name".
  - Some vessels carry both: an interior mark made before firing (potter) and an exterior mark made after firing (owner).
- **Inscriptions described only in words** (pp. 218–220):
  - **Bone piece**, RGR-2, trench Y 24/4, Mature Harappan: seven signs described as "I N I X I X X". It is the only multi-sign text in the chapter.
  - **Lead piece**, RGR-2, trench Q-19/4, Mature Harappan: on one side, 5 strokes in a row plus 1 below; on the other side, 8 strokes. The excavator suggests "used as a seal".
  - **Terracotta cakes** (7): single signs, including an arrowhead and a cross with a stroke.
  - **Terracotta beads** (2): a "tree" sign, and an oval with strokes and a cross.
- **Seals.** The report mentions "a few square seals of steatite ... bearing Indus sign" (p. 107) and an unfinished seal, RGR 1731 (p. 368). No catalogue of seal texts.

**Verdict.** No sign-level data can be extracted. The drawings would sit in the ASI archive, either the final report (if one was ever issued) or the excavation records. The 163-sherd context table could support a pre- vs post-firing and vessel-part analysis only once the marks are available.
