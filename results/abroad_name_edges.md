# Do sign groups written abroad repeat at their edges, like names with a god's name? (scouting)

*26 October 2026. Script `scripts/abroad_name_edges.py`, output `abroad_name_edges_output.txt`. All 42 abroad texts (already seen), so this is description, not a test.*

**Idea (Erin's "different lens").** Semitic personal names of the period repeat a few elements (gods' names such as "-El") at one edge, while the rest varies. If the Gulf and abroad texts write such names, the first or last sign of each sign group (split at man signs) should repeat more than in home texts of the same length.

| Group edge | Repetition abroad | Home samples (5,000), mean and 95% range | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| First sign | 0.13 | 0.20 (0.10–0.32) | 0.91 |
| Last sign | 0.34 | 0.48 (0.35–0.62) | 0.98 |
| Both | 0.36 | 0.38 (0.29–0.47) | 0.68 |

**Result: not supported. Edges repeat *less* abroad than at home.** Home texts end in fixed closing signs (above all the jar G740), and abroad texts do so less. That fits the earlier finding that abroad texts break the home formula (`abroad_pairs_test_2026-10-23.md`).

Abroad, edges are more varied: one-off groups, as personal names would be. No repeating "-El"-like element shows up across texts, though 42 texts from many places, times and possibly languages can only show a strong effect.

**The one sign that does repeat at the start of groups abroad is twelve (G55): 4 times, more than any other sign.** The jar G740 is the commonest group-final sign (8).
