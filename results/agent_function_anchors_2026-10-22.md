# Function anchors for the Indus script: inscribed objects whose purpose is known

Compiled 2026-10-22 for the Indus-Research- project (Erin Rae Toppe).

**Rule used here:** I did not invent any sign sequence. Where a source gives catalogue numbers (CISI numbers such as H-/M-/L-, Mahadevan 1977 numbers, or Parpola sign numbers), they are copied as the source gives them. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from memory, or from a secondary or informal source, and were not checked against a primary publication in this session.

**Numbering systems used below:**
- CISI = Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions (Joshi & Parpola 1987; Shah & Parpola 1991; Parpola, Pande & Koskikallio 2010). It gives object numbers such as H-1727, M-2097 and L-208.
- M77 = Mahadevan 1977 concordance. It gives text numbers (e.g. #2923) and sign numbers (1–417).
- P-sign = Parpola 1994 sign list (fig. 5.1).

## 1. Weights, scales, capacity vessels

### 1a. Weights: the negative result is verified
- **Finding:** Indus cubical chert and stone weights are **not inscribed**. harappa.com ("Ancient Indus Weights") says the weights are "unfortunately not inscribed with any Indus characters."
- **Where else weights turn up:** Kenoyer & Meadow 2010 note standardized cuboid weights from the Kot Diji Phase onward, with no inscriptions mentioned. Weights also occur in the Gulf (Tell Abraq ×3; Shimal ×2, in graves), and Frenez 2018 records no inscriptions on them.
- **What this means:** No weight-to-text anchor exists. Weight-text links can only be indirect, through seals found together with weights. Mukhopadhyay 2023 reports seals found with standardized weights in Mohenjo-daro public buildings.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.harappa.com/blog/ancient-indus-weights
  - https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The_Indus_Civilization_Trade_with_the_Om.pdf (p. 393)
- **Reliability:** High.

### 1b. Measuring scales [UNVERIFIED]
- **Objects:** The Lothal ivory scale, the Mohenjo-daro shell scale and the Harappa bronze rod.
- **Finding:** As far as I know, they carry graduation marks only, with no script. I found no source in this session that reports script on any of them.

### 1c. Wells's "volumetric" storage pots from Harappa (partly verified)
- **Objects:** Three clay pots from the old Harappa excavations, held in New Delhi.
  - The smallest is marked with a "V"-like sign (Wells: "measure") and 3 long strokes.
  - The second has 6 strokes.
  - The largest has 7 strokes, is about 2.7 m in circumference and holds about 65 L.
- **Wells's claim:**
  - The pots' capacities are in the ratio 3:6:7, matching the stroke counts.
  - One long stroke ≈ 9.24 L (≈10 L).
  - The same "V + strokes" groups occur on bas-relief tablets, which he reads as ration chits.
- **What the function implies:** If the claim holds, then **long stroke = a numeral, and the "V"/pot-like sign = a unit of capacity.** This is the single best capacity anchor proposed so far.
- **Sign identity:** Press reports call the sign "V". Rao 2018 calls it a "vessel-like symbol" (≈40 L in Wells's scheme). Wells-list numbers were not retrieved.
- **Sources:**
  - Stone Pages news, 23 Nov 2009 (summarizing The Hindu): https://www.stonepages.com/news/archives/003607.html
  - Varnam summary: https://www.varnam.org/2009/12/03/the-harappan-volumetric-system/
  - Rao 2018, "The Indus Script and Economics," in *Walking with the Unicorn*: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00049
  - Full treatment is in B. Wells 2015, *The Archaeology and Epigraphy of Indus Writing* (Archaeopress) [not read].
- **Reliability:** Medium-low. The measurements are real per the press reports, but there are only 3 vessels and the claim was never peer-reviewed as a stand-alone paper. **It is testable:** check the pot numbers in CISI vol. 1 or 3 and measure the capacities.

### 1d. Standard-volume vessel: Indus black-slipped jar (BSJ)
- **Object:** A standardized export jar, c. 70 cm tall and c. 30–40 L in capacity, made at Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (Méry & Blackman 1999). Residue work had not been done when they wrote, so the contents are unknown.
- **Text:** In Oman (about 1000 fragments known), the jars "occasionally" carry "a short sequence of Indus script signs" scratched on the rim or upper body. Example: Ras al-Jinz RJ-2, Building II, Room 18 (Frenez 2018, fig. 35.2b).
  - Whether the signs were cut before or after firing is not stated in the sources read.
  - Sign sequences were **not retrieved**.
- **What the function implies:**
  - A pre-firing text would most likely name the producer or workshop.
  - A post-firing text would most likely name the contents, the consignee or a quantity.
  - Because the volume is standard, a numeral would be unnecessary unless it counted jars.
- **Sources:**
  - Frenez 2018, in Cleuziou & Tosi, *In the Shadow of the Ancestors*, Window 35, p. 386: https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The_Indus_Civilization_Trade_with_the_Om.pdf
  - Méry & Blackman 1999, *Paléorient* 25/2: https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/d3aa7120-2c22-4d25-8806-29ed6dfe0ecb/content
- **Reliability:** The objects are high-reliability. The texts still need a primary publication: Cleuziou & Tosi 2000 (RJ-2), and the Méry & Blackman "Black jars of Meluhha" paper.

### 1e. Harappa Phase storage jars
- **Object:** harappa.com: "Many large storage jars of the Harappa Phase (2600–1900 BCE) have writing inscribed along the upper portion of the vessel." The example shown is a compound sign: a man holding a bow-and-arrow sign in each hand.
- **Related find:** A fragment of a mold used to make large storage jars carries 3 pre-firing signs (c. 2600 BCE). harappa.com suggests the signs name an owner.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.harappa.com/slide/inscribed-storage-jar
  - https://www.harappa.com/blog/storage-jar-script
  - https://www.harappa.com/slide/inscribed-mold-fragment
- **Reliability:** The objects are high-reliability. Sign numbers were not retrieved.

## 2. Tools, ornaments, rods, pottery of a fixed type

### 2a. Pointed-base goblets (PBGs) stamped before firing at Harappa (Period 3C). STRONG ANCHOR
- **Object:** Mass-produced, single-use drinking cups ("tens if not hundreds of thousands").
- **Text:** A "very small percentage" carry a **script stamp applied before firing**, made with the long rectangular seals that occur only in Period 3C.
  - CISI H-1727 to H-1766 are stamped fragments; H-1081 and H-1082 are complete goblets.
  - **Six sherds at Lahoma Lal Tibba (about 24 km ESE) carry the same stamps as H-1756 to H-1759.** This is the same text on the same vessel type at two sites.
  - Some Mohenjo-daro goblets also carry a seal impression on the shoulder or base (harappa.com slide).
- **What the function implies:**
  - The text was applied at the pottery workshop, before firing, to a disposable cup. It is therefore either a **producer, workshop or authority mark**, or a mark of the **issuing institution or occasion**, such as a ration or a feast.
  - It is unlikely to be the drinker's personal name, given the mass production. Kenoyer suggests "specific owners or specific rituals."
- **Sources:**
  - Kenoyer & Meadow 2010, CISI vol. 3, pp. xliv–lviii: https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer-Meadow-2010-HARP.pdf
  - https://www.harappa.com/slide/pointed-base-goblets
- **Reliability:** High for the facts.
- **Next step:** Pull the H-1727–H-1766 sequences from CISI vol. 3 and check how many distinct texts exist and whether they share signs with seals.

### 2b. Ivory and bone rods (Parpola 2020). STRONG REPEATED-TEXT DATASET
- **Object:** About 32 inscribed rod texts, 26 of them readable. Function uncertain: dice or divination sticks (Kenoyer, Miller), or personal objects.
- **Duplicates:** Texts 4, 5, 6, 7, 16 and 22 each occur on **two rods**.
  - Text 4 = M-1651, M-2106
  - Text 5 = M-2107, M-2108
  - Text 6 = M-2086, M-2102
  - Text 7 = M-2109, M-2111
  - Text 16 = M-2089, M-2090
  - Text 22 = M-2096, H-1517
- **Cross-type parallels** (the same text on objects of other kinds):
  - Text 14 (M-2097) = incised tablets H-311 to H-318 (reverse "VII, VIII or VIIII" in Parpola's notation) and prism tablets H-352 to H-354.
  - Text 17 (M-1017) = identical incised tablets M-501, M-1444, M-1445, M-2040, M-2041.
  - Text 15 (M-2091) = bar seal H-670, faience tablet H-1808, and moulded tablets H-189, H-190, H-724–727, H-1104–1106.
  - Text 9 (M-1650) is closest to the pottery graffito H-1398.
- **Signs (P-sign numbers):**
  - P-107 "comb" ends many rod texts. It is present or absent in otherwise identical texts, and Parpola suspects a feminine marker.
  - P-225 and P-40 (a sign and its "man"-ligature) are interchangeable.
  - P-91 is preceded by a numeral, and "P-91 × 4" equals "4 + P-91" (text 22 versus text 23). This is direct evidence of **numeral + counted noun** syntax.
- **Source:** Parpola 2020, in Myrdal (ed.), *South Asian Archaeology and Art 2014*, pp. 71–76: https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/5994c972-f2c5-4520-954d-8a9b63d27b25/content. Miller on the rods: https://www.harappa.com/content/new-ivory-rods-mohenjo-daro-and-exploration-object-class
- **Reliability:** High for catalogue facts. The function of the rods is uncertain. The numeral equivalence is a structural fact worth testing in the app.

### 2c. Incised and moulded tablets with numeral + "pot" sign on the reverse (Harappa) [interpretive]
- **Object:** Parpola reads the reverse sequences, such as H-311 to H-318, as counts of offerings ("UIII" = three pots in his notation) and the tablets as receipts or amulets given to people making offerings.
- **Large duplicate sets** (Kenoyer & Meadow 2010):
  - 22 incised steatite tablets, H-2218 to H-2239, from Trench 11, Mound E.
  - 31 molded terracotta duplicates from Area G, south of the Mound ET gateway: H-252–265, H-276–277, H-859–870, H-1155.
  - All were found in trash, fill or street deposits.
- **What the function implies:** If the tablets are tokens or receipts, the text = the issuer or transaction type + a quantity.
- **Sources:**
  - Kenoyer & Meadow 2010 (link above)
  - https://www.harappa.com/slide/tokens-or-tablets-harappa
  - Parpola 2020 (link above)
- **Reliability:** High for the duplicates. The reading of their function is interpretive.

### 2d. Inscribed copper axe, Mohenjo-daro [contested reading]
- **Object:** A copper axe with a damaged inscription.
  - Mackay 1938, pl. 126:2 and 131:31.
  - M77 text #2923 (side 1, p. 85).
  - Parpola 1994: 108 draws it as a 7-sign sequence that recurs on "a series of Mohenjo-daro seals, sealings, and weapons" and ends in what he calls a "royal title."
- **Problem:** S. Farmer shows that Mackay, M77 and Parpola give three different versions, and that Parpola's drawing adds signs Mackay did not see.
- **What the function implies:** An owner's name or title on a weapon.
- **Source:** https://safarmer.com/indus-reconstructions-axehead/
- **Reliability:** LOW for the sequence. Use only the signs that all three drawings agree on.

### 2e. Inscribed lead celt or ingot, Harappa
- **Object:** H2000-4481/2174-321, from the Trench 54 area, apparently chiselled to reduce its size.
- **What the function implies:** On a metal ingot, the text = the metal or commodity, its weight, or the owner or supplier.
- **Source:** https://www.harappa.com/slide/inscribed-fragment
- **Reliability:** High for the object. The sign sequence was not retrieved.

### 2f. Potter's bat with a pre-firing inscription, Harappa (Trench 54)
- **Object:** A bat for a potter's wheel, inscribed under the ring base before firing.
- **What the function implies:** A potter's or workshop mark on a potter's own tool. Compare it with the PBG stamps and storage-jar marks from the same workshops.
- **Source:** harappa.com "writing" slides: https://www.harappa.com/category/slide-subject/writing?page=1
- **Reliability:** High for the object. The sequence was not retrieved.

### 2g. Stoneware bangles (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro)
- **Object:**
  - A standardized type: inner diameter about 5.5–6 cm, fired at very high temperature, produced only in small numbers.
  - Tiny script signs on the outer edge.
  - Kenoyer reads them as a "badge of office or ritual ornament."
  - Vidale and colleagues reconstructed a controlled, multi-container firing process.
  - Frenez (Mirrored Signs, fig. 3D) illustrates a **sealed clay coating on a composite jar used to fire stoneware bangles at Mohenjo-daro**. In other words, firing loads were sealed: an administrative seal on a production batch.
- **What the function implies:**
  - Bangle text = the rank or office of the wearer, or the issuing authority.
  - Sealing on the firing jar = the authority controlling production.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.harappa.com/slide/stoneware-bangle (Kenoyer, *Ancient Cities* pp. 145–46)
  - Blackman & Vidale 1992, SAA 1989: https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/43393
  - Halim & Vidale 1984, "Kilns, bangles and coated vessels" [not read]
  - Vidale 2024, *Man & Environment*: https://research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3502969
  - Kenoyer 2021, painted script incl. bangles: https://www.harappa.com/content/painted-indus-script-ceramics-and-steatite-new-insights-indus-script-calligraphy-and
  - Frenez: https://www.academia.edu/41634804
- **Reliability:** High for the objects. **No bangle sign sequences were retrieved.** Whether the same text repeats across bangles is [UNVERIFIED]. Fuls's corpus has a BNGL category, so it can be queried.

### 2h. Gold pendant, Mohenjo-daro
- **Object:** An inscription noticed by Kenoyer (2021) that earlier excavators had missed.
- **Source:** Kenoyer 2021, *Ancient Pakistan* 32 (link above).
- **Reliability:** Medium (summary only). Sequence not retrieved.

### 2i. Copper tablets, Mohenjo-daro [UNVERIFIED in this session; from Parpola 1994]
- **Object:** About 240 small incised copper tablets.
- **Pattern:** Tablets fall into types in which a given motif on one side goes with a given text on the other, so the same text is repeated across many tablets of a type.
- **Function:** Unknown (amulets or tokens).
- **Why it matters:** This is the best "same text, same object class" set at Mohenjo-daro, but it ties text to an image, not to a known purpose.
- **Reliability:** The pattern is widely reported. Verify it in CISI vol. 1 and Parpola 1994, ch. 7.

## 3. Sealings with known closures

### 3a. Lothal warehouse sealings (Frenez 2024). STRONG CLOSURE-TYPE DATASET
- **Count:** 93 clay sealings in all; about 70 in one cluster (about 1 m²) in the burnt "warehouse" (Blocks 41l/41j, SRG 3, GX 10). Frenez argues they had been removed from containers and stored, i.e. an archive.
- **Closure types identified from the impressions on the back** (fig. 9, CISI L-numbers):
  - doors: L-179, L-206
  - wooden cage: L-190
  - wooden lockers or structures: L-143, L-144, L-148
  - wooden box: L-163
  - pottery vessel: L-177
  - rope: L-208
  - Also leather or cloth bags, and reed containers.
- **Repeated seals:**
  - Excavated ivory seal L-6 stamped L-201.
  - An undiscovered seal stamped L-199, L-201 and L-208.
  - Another undiscovered seal stamped L-207 and L-208.
  - Seal L-37 stamped L-210.
  - A single sealing carries up to 5 different seal impressions. Frenez reads this as several officials or authorities endorsing one closure.
- **Same text on several sealings:** Mukhopadhyay 2023 reports that one sign sequence recurs on several Lothal sealings and also on Mohenjo-daro seal M-128. She also says **the same inscription occurs on containers of different sizes**, so the text does not encode quantity. Sequence not transcribed in the extracted text: [UNVERIFIED sequence].
- **What the function implies:** The text = the issuer or authority (office, title, name), not the contents. A text that appears on doors and lockers as well as on movable containers would be an office; a text tied only to one container type might be a commodity.
- **Sources:**
  - Frenez 2024, *South Asian Studies* 40(2): 214–234, doi:10.1080/02666030.2024.2401206: https://www.academia.edu/127571731
  - harappa.com summary: https://www.harappa.com/content/were-cretulae-clay-sealings-indus-port-town-lothal-part-administrative-archive-contextual
  - Mukhopadhyay 2023, *Humanities & Social Sciences Communications* 10: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02320-7
- **Reliability:** High for the closure types and links.
- **Next step:** Get the L-sealing texts (CISI vol. 1 or 2, Gujarat section) and tabulate text against closure type. This is the most direct test available.

### 3b. Mohenjo-daro tags M-425 and M-426
- **M-425:** One tag with impressions of several different square and rectangular inscribed seals.
- **M-426:** Stamped twice by the same seals, with **Indus signs scratched on the back after removal from the container**. This is a second-stage annotation, possibly a docket of what was opened or checked.
- **Source:** Joshi & Parpola 1987: 104–105, via Frenez, "Mirrored signs": https://www.academia.edu/41634804
- **Reliability:** High.
- **Next step:** The scratched text on M-426 should be compared with the seal texts on the front.

### 3c. Harappa sealings
- **Objects:**
  - H-1719 to H-1724: six tags.
  - H-1725: an unbaked sub-conical **jar-stopper** with a seal impression.
- **Source:** Kenoyer & Meadow 2010 (link above).
- **Reliability:** High. Texts not retrieved.

### 3d. Kanmer (Gujarat) clay pendants
- **Object:** Three round, perforated baked-clay pieces. **All three carry an impression of the same unicorn seal, and each has a different single Indus sign on the reverse.** Osada suggests they were passes or "passports." Mukhopadhyay's summary also mentions a wild-ass seal at the site.
- **What the function implies:** One authority (one seal) plus a variable one-sign field. The single sign = a serial, a category, a destination or a holder class. **This is a clean natural experiment on what a single sign does.**
- **Source:** https://www.harappa.com/blog/ancient-indus-passports. Primary: Kharakwal, Rawat & Osada (eds.), *Excavations at Kanmer* [not read].
- **Reliability:** High for the facts. The signs were not retrieved.

### 3e. Salut ST1 (Oman): a local jar stamped twice with the same inscribed tablet
- **Object:** Two fragments of one local jar, each stamped with the same elongated tablet: two wild bison in combat plus several Indus signs. A similar moulded tablet exists at Harappa.
- **What the function implies:** An Indus trader or authority marking a container in Oman.
- **Source:** Frenez 2018, p. 393, fig. 35.12; Frenez et al. 2016: 117–118, fig. 10 (link above).
- **Reliability:** High. Sequence not retrieved.

### 3f. Method note: Fuls's corpus classifies tags by closure
- **Categories:** palm sealings, cube sealings, **pot-rim sealings**, **textile/reed sealings**, **wooden strip/pole sealings**, **box sealings**.
- **Use:** The project can query ICIT or Fuls's *Corpus of Indus Inscriptions* (2022) for "tag text × closure type" directly.
- **Source:** https://www.academia.edu/83046175

## 4. Known architectural or workshop contexts

### 4a. Dholavira signboard
- **Object:** 10 large gypsum-inlay signs set in a wooden board about 3 m long. It was found in the western chamber of the North Gate of the Castle, probably fitted above the lintel. One sign occurs 4 times.
- **Sequence:** As given in a reader comment on Varnam (presumably Mahadevan M77 numbers): **391, 256, 327, 391, 261, 134, 98, 391, 391, 53** [UNVERIFIED: the system is not stated and it is not from a primary source]. It does agree with Bisht that one sign is repeated 4 times.
- **What the function implies:** A gate inscription should name the city, the gate or ward, or the ruler or institution (Bisht). If the repeated sign also appears often in seal texts, it is a candidate for a title or a frequent morpheme.
- **Other finds at Dholavira:** A stone block with 4 signs from an underground chamber in the Bailey, possibly from a door lintel, and a slab fragment with 3 large signs.
- **Sources:**
  - Bisht 2015 report: https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Dholavira-Bisht2015.pdf
  - https://www.harappa.com/slide/dholavira-symbols
  - https://www.varnam.org/?p=2783
- **Reliability:** High for the context. Verify the sequence in CISI vol. 3 or Parpola 1994.

### 4b. Workshops [partial]
- **Harappa, Mound E workshop:** A steatite and faience tablet workshop (Kenoyer 2005, cited in Mukhopadhyay 2023).
- **Harappa, Trench 54:** The potter's bat and the lead ingot both come from here.
- **Lothal and Chanhu-daro:** Bead workshops yielded seals, but no text-to-craft link is published that I could verify.
- **Mohenjo-daro, DK-G:** Mukhopadhyay reports one collocation repeated on seals with different animals (M-753, M-1693, M-781, M-1079, M-1890, M-276, M-1155, M-1169). This supports the project's finding that text is independent of the seal animal.
- **Mohenjo-daro, HR-A House I:** Seven unicorn seals with different texts.
- **Source:** Mukhopadhyay 2023 (link above).
- **Reliability:** Medium. The catalogue numbers are from a peer-reviewed paper, but the interpretation is hers.
