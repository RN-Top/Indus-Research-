Compiled 29 October 2026 for the Indus-Research- project (Erin Rae Toppe). This is a literature search, not a test.

**Marks.** [V] means verified from a page I fetched in this session, with the URL and a short quote. [U] means unverified: it comes from memory, from a secondary summary I could not check, or from a fetch that returned only a paraphrase.

Where I add new detail to one of these, I say so.

## PART 1. Substrate words in the Rigveda and early Sanskrit

### 1.1 How sure anyone is that these are "Harappan"

- **Witzel's label is provisional.**
  - [V] "I rather prefer the provisional name, Para-Munda (or simply, Harappan)" (fn. 16).
  - [V] He claims the Panjab only: "the language of the Panjab was Para-Munda already during the Indus period" (§9).
  - [V] For Sindh, "results must remain very tentative for the moment" (§11).
  - Source: Witzel, *The Languages of Harappa* (2000 draft), https://d-nb.info/1218975474/34
- **How many words are at stake.**
  - [V] "some 4% of the words in this sacred text are clearly of non-IE, nonIndo-Aryan origin"
  - [V] "Kuiper (1991) lists 383"
  - [V] "we still retain some 211-250 'foreign' words" (same URL)
- **Fuller (2007) separates Language X from the Harappan language.**
  - [V] He places Language X as "more upper Gangetic centred", which he "previously suggested might be linked to the Neolithic of the Ganges valley" (p. 416).
  - [V] He keeps "the Harappan (Kubha-Vipās) tradition" as a separate substrate (p. 420).
  - Source: Fuller 2007, "Non-human genetics, agricultural origins and historical linguistics in South Asia", in Petraglia & Allchin (eds.), https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~tcrndfu/articles/Fuller%20in%20Petraglia&Allchin.pdf
  - **Consequence:** Masica's "Language X" words are *not* good Indus anchors on Fuller's reading.
- **Southworth (2005) assigns the Indus Valley Para-Munda only by paraphrasing Witzel and Kuiper.**
  - [V] "Para-Munda was a major language of the Indus Valley culture" (ch. 10, paraphrasing them)
  - [V] "Witzel (1999b:14) even suggests that it may have been the language of the Indus script"
  - Source: Southworth, *Linguistic Archaeology of South Asia*, contents/excerpt PDF, https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/~fsouth/LASAcontents.pdf
- **Parpola excludes the BMAC layer.** [V] "Hence these words do not represent the Harappan language" (p. 11). This refers to the Central Asian/BMAC loans: *aṃśu*, *iṣṭakā* 'brick', *uṣṭra* 'camel', *śaṇa* 'hemp' and so on. Source: Parpola 2010, "A Dravidian solution to the Indus script problem", https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Parpola-2010-Coimbatore.pdf
  - Keep the BMAC words out of any Indus anchor list. The Wikipedia list of them was fetched, but I do not repeat it.
- **Van Driem calls para-Munda "necessarily nebulous".** [V] The source is MT-SPECL Oct 1999, critiquing Witzel: https://www.isw.unibe.ch/ueber_uns/e41180/e523709/e547102/1999d_ger.pdf
- **Southworth & McAlpin.** The only page I reached was Southworth's Cornell "rice and language" abstract.
  - [V] It claims "strong new evidence to support the Elamo-Dravidian (Proto-Zagrosian) hypothesis". https://conf.ling.cornell.edu/riceandlanguage/abstracts/Southworth_abstract.pdf
  - It gives no words. Their joint Zagrosian word list is [U]: not found.

### 1.3 Assessment
- **Pictured objects.** Of the words with a fairly secure Indus-area substrate claim (*tila*, *karpāsa*, *khajūra*, *kulmāṣa*, *kīnāśa*, *kuliśa*, *karambha*), none names an object clearly pictured in the sign list. The candidates that come closest are the vessel (*kalaśa*, *kabandha*), whose source is disputed, and the axe (*kuliśa*), whose sign is not mapped.
- **Dravidian-only animals.** The animal words that do match Indus pictures, *mayūra* peacock and the elephant forms, are proposed as Dravidian rather than Para-Munda.

## PART 2. Cuneiform: new Meluhha-linked words (not in the 22 Oct file)

### 2.1 Witzel's count of about 40 "Indian" words in Mesopotamia
- [V] "In total, there are some 40 "Indian" words transmitted to ancient Mesopotamia," LoH §11, https://d-nb.info/1218975474/34
  - Witzel gives no full list. The nearest citation in that passage is **Possehl 1996a** ("Meluhha", in J. Reade (ed.), *The Indian Ocean in Antiquity*). That bibliographic record was found at https://afo.orient.univie.ac.at/en/detailed-entry/?pdb=121060 but not read.
- **Action:** The Possehl 1996a list is the single most useful document to obtain. [U]: I could not reach its content.

### 2.2 Sindh wood, *sindû*
- **Witzel's claim.** [V] "Sindh wood sinda (si-in-da-a, si-in-du)" is one of his "Indian" words in Mesopotamia (LoH §11).
- **Streck's caution.** [V] Streck's RlA entry "Sind A" is cautious. Its fetch was paraphrased from the German, roughly: whether the wood written si-in-da-a in Sennacherib's inscriptions belongs here is unclear. It cites AHw and CAD s.v. *sindû*, and a variant "si-in-du" (Borger, ARRIM 6, 1988). https://www.gko.uni-leipzig.de/fileadmin/Fakult%C3%A4t_GKR/Altorientalisches_Institut/Dokumente/Institut/Mitarbeiter/Streck_Dokumente/RlA/RlA_Sind.pdf
- **Date.** This is Neo-Assyrian (Sennacherib, about 700 BC), so far too late for Harappan.
- **Value:** low. It is a place-derived name, not a Harappan word.

### 2.3 gišimmar ~ Vedic *śimbala* / *śalmali* (Witzel 2001)
- **The proposal.** Witzel published the only paper I found that claims a specific Para-Munda (Indus) word inside Mesopotamian vocabulary:
  - "A first link between the Ṛgvedic Panjab and Mesopotamia: śimbala/śalmali and GIŠgišimmar?", in Karttunen & Koskikallio (eds.), *Vidyārṇavavandanam: Essays in Honour of Asko Parpola*, *Studia Orientalia* 94 (2001): 497–508.
  - [V] Bibliographic record: https://indianmedicine.eldoc.ub.rug.nl/3053/. Wikipedia "Harappan language" notes: "An Indus loanword of "para-Munda" nature in Mesopotamian has been identified by Michael Witzel".
  - [V] In LoH he lists "the gišgišimmar wood (cf. \*śimmal in Ved. śimbala" among Meluhhan(?) trees coming via Dilmun.
- **Not read.** The paper's own argument was not read (journal.fi blocked).
- **A cautionary flag (mine, [U]).** In standard Sumerian, giš-gišimmar is the ordinary word for the date palm, attested from very early. Specialist reaction to Witzel's proposal is unknown to me.
- **Value:** moderate as a methodological model, but low as a sign anchor. *Bombax* is not pictured.

### 2.4 aluppu / giš-a-lu-ub wood
- [V] "Coming from Dilmun (Bahrain), we may add the Meluhhan(?) trees giš-a-lu-ub or aluppu wood," (LoH §11)
- Witzel's own question mark stands. **Value:** low.

### 2.5 "zaza cattle (zebu?)"
- [V] "zaza cattle (zebu?), elephants, etc." (LoH §11)
- The Sumerian or Akkadian spelling, the text and the date are [U], and Witzel himself marks the zebu identification with "?".
- **Why it matters:** zebu is a seal motif. The project corpus has 44 inscriptions with the `Zebu` motif.

### 2.6 Elephant: Akkadian *pīru/pīri*, Old Persian *pīru-* 'ivory' < Proto-Dravidian \*pīlu (Mukhopadhyay 2021)
- **The claim.** [V] The elephant words in Bronze Age Mesopotamia, the elephant word in the Hurrian part of an Amarna letter, and the Old Persian ivory word were "all originally borrowed from 'pīlu', a Proto-Dravidian elephant-word". This is derived from PD \*pal / \*pīl 'tooth'.
- **A second link.** Skt *pīlu* (the toothbrush tree, *Salvadora persica*) is linked to the Indus valley.
- **Source.** Mukhopadhyay, B. A. 2021, *Humanities & Social Sciences Communications* 8: 193, doi:10.1057/s41599-021-00868-w. Preview at https://paperity.org/p/273902947/ancestral-dravidian-languages-in-indus-civilization-ultraconserved-dravidian-tooth-word
- **Press summary.** [V] Scroll.in gives the forms "pīru'/'pīri" (Akkadian) and "pīrus" (Old Persian, ivory), and notes that the paper "ignores the script in her analysis". https://scroll.in/article/1002571/how-the-mesopotamian-word-for-elephant-proves-dravidian-language-existed-in-indus-civilisation
- **Unchecked details.** The exact cuneiform attestations, their dates and their CAD references were not seen [U]. Assyriological acceptance is [U].
- **Not new to the 22 Oct file?** That file mentions *pīru* only as a one-word grade-C candidate. This is the first sourced argument.
- **Value: HIGH relative to the rest,** because the project already has an **elephant sign, G706**. A copper tablet pairs its text with an elephant image: `G706 G33 G923 G740 → elephant` (see `results/copper_sets.md`, `results/copper_image_test.md`). That gives a sign with a known referent and a proposed word with a proposed sound.

### 2.7 *Enki and the World Order*: the full Meluhha blessing (new detail beyond l. 126)
- **The passage.** [V] ETCSL 1.1.3, lines 219–237, addressed to Meluhha as "Black land": https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section1/tr113.htm
  - "may your forests be forests of highland *mes* trees!"
  - "May your reeds be great reeds"
  - "May your bulls be great bulls, may they be bulls of the mountains!"
  - "May the francolins of the mountains wear cornelian beards!"
  - "May your birds all be peacocks!"
- **The peacock word.** [V] RlA "Vogel" §6.6 lists **ḫa-ia "peacock"** under bird names formed from **cries**, citing Veldhuis 2004 and *Nanše and the Birds* l. 50, where the cry is "ḫa-ia ḫa-ia". https://www.gko.uni-leipzig.de/fileadmin/Fakult%C3%A4t_GKR/Altorientalisches_Institut/Dokumente/Institut/Mitarbeiter/Streck_Dokumente/RlA/RlA_Vogel.pdf
  - **Implication:** the Sumerian peacock word is onomatopoeic. It is **not** evidence for a Meluhhan loan. Drop "peacock" from the loanword candidates on the cuneiform side.
- **Rooster.** [V] The same RlA entry gives "tarlu-gallu < dar-lugal, lit. "royal francolin", "rooster"". This is a Sumerian coinage, not a Meluhhan word, even though the chicken itself is an Indus-region animal.

### 2.8 Sesame: Sumerian *ili* (new detail beyond the 22 Oct grade-C row)
- **Witzel.** [V] "Sumer. ili 'sesame', Akkad. ellu/ūlu 'sesame oil'", to be "compared, however, also with Ved. tila and jar-tila" (LoH §11). Elsewhere he says the source of *(t)il* is "unclear".
- **Witzel 2009.** [V] "one must not forget the Mesopotamian word for sesame, ellu" (W09 p. 15).
- **Southworth.** [V] South Dravidian *eḷḷu* 'sesame' "has been linked to Akkadian ellu sesame", and the trade suggestion is "between the Indus Valley and Mesopotamia in Harappan times" (SPDA note 31).
- **Status.** This is the best-documented trade word shared by the three areas. The direction of borrowing is open.

### 2.9 magilum boats "of Meluhhan style"
- [V] Witzel: "magilum boats of Meluan style (Possehl 1996a)." The paperity preview of Mukhopadhyay 2021 says Parpola treats *magilum* as a suspect Indic word in Mesopotamian texts [V-p]. The original Parpola citation is [U].
- The 22 Oct file gives only the *Enki* attestation. The loanword claim is new here.
- **Value:** low. There is no boat sign in common use. Boat images occur on a few tablets, which the project could check.

### 2.10 Searched and not found (reported so they are not re-searched)
- **Rice and cotton.** No third-millennium cuneiform word for rice or cotton argued to be Meluhhan was found.
- **Wikipedia "Meluhha".** It says only that cotton textiles and chickens were Indus products "not native to Mesopotamia". [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meluhha
- **Water buffalo.** I found no loanword for water buffalo. The Ibni-šarrum seal is iconography only and is already in the 22 Oct file.

- None of the proposals above has been tested by its author against held-out data.
- Parpola's method is cross-checking by "interlocking of consistent readings with each other and with external linguistic data" (p. 18) [V]. It is consistency, not prediction.
- The project's own runs (fish + jar + man, Supported; crab + fish not reproduced for G140) are the only out-of-sample checks I know of.

## PART 4. Ranked anchor candidates and the data that would test each

### 1. Elephant: sign G706 ~ Akkadian *pīru/pīri* ~ PD \*pīl- (Mukhopadhyay 2021)
- **Why first.**
  - It is the only case with all three links: a sign whose referent is fixed by a picture pairing (copper tablet: G706 … → elephant image), a cuneiform word for the same animal, and a published claim that the cuneiform word came from the Indus language.
- **Test against the corpus.**
  1. **Logogram test.** G706 should behave as a content word. Earlier results show it sits medially (26 of 36), inside the recurring unit G706 G33, and not on elephant-motif seals (0 of 24).
     - Check that G706 never takes the slot positions occupied by the suffix-like signs (G760, G100 and so on).
     - Check that the elephant pairing holds on *all* copper tablets that carry G706.
  2. **Rebus test** (only if a phonetic hypothesis exists). If G706 = *pīl-*, its uses on non-elephant seals should be explicable as *pīl-* homophones ('tooth', 'split'; *pīlu* the tree).
     - This needs an independently fixed sound for at least one neighbouring sign, otherwise it is circular.
- **External data needed.**
  - The full cuneiform attestations of *pīru* with dates (CAD P; check the earliest Old Akkadian / Ur III occurrence).
  - The full text of Mukhopadhyay 2021, from nature.com.
  - A specialist assessment of whether *pīru* can be explained from Semitic or Elamite.

### 2. Fish: G220 = *mīn* 'fish/star' (Parpola)
- **Why second.** It is the most-developed sign–word proposal, and the project already has the mapping. Its weakness is that the only check is internal consistency.
- **Test.** Mahadevan's own criterion: find *mīn*-type sequences where the fish sign is replaced by a phonetic spelling.
  - Operationally: look for sign pairs that substitute for G220 in otherwise identical texts. The project's `spelling_swaps.md` machinery fits this.
  - If a substituting pair exists, its signs should be reusable elsewhere as *mi-* / *-n*.
  - Also predict, before looking, the relative frequency of numeral + fish combinations. Old Tamil has named asterisms for 6 and 7; check whether other numerals before the fish are rarer.
- **Data.** ICIT or the project corpus with the held-out set (already available). No new external data is needed.

### 3. Arrow = *ampu*, a non-masculine suffix (Mahadevan)
- **Why third.** It is a phonetic plus grammatical proposal that makes *distributional* predictions, testable without decoding anything else.
- **Test** (pre-register):
  - (a) The arrow sign should be text-final or unit-final at rates like the jar sign.
  - (b) Arrow and jar should be in **complementary distribution** after the same preceding sign groups. As gender suffixes, the same name-stem should not take both.
  - (c) The arrow sign should follow a different set of stems from the man-sign endings (G90), if the man sign marks male title-holders.
- **Data.**
  - Map Mahadevan's ARROW sign number to a G-number through `crosswalk_G_to_P.csv` or Fuls. It is not yet mapped in the project; no G-number turned up in `results/`.
  - Then use the locked holdout.

### 4. Sesame and oil: Sum. *ili* / Akk. *ellu* ~ SDr *eḷḷu* ~ Skt *tila* / *jar-tila*
- **Why fourth.** It is the best-documented shared trade word across the three areas, and Meluhhans are recorded receiving sesame oil (22 Oct file, §2.1). But no sesame picture-sign exists, so any test has to go through containers.
- **Test.** Check whether inscriptions on vessels with residue evidence for plant oil share a sign group absent from vessels with other contents. The Umma jar sealing and the Ras al-Jinz inscribed jars are candidates.
- **Data needed.** Published lipid-residue results for **inscribed** Harappan vessels or their jar types, matched to the inscription IDs in the project corpus. I found none in this search, so that literature still needs a dedicated search.

### 5. Zebu, buffalo and other motif animals: "zaza cattle (zebu?)" (Witzel / Possehl 1996a)
- **Why fifth.** The cuneiform side is weak (Witzel's own "?"; the spelling and text are unverified). But the Indus side is strong: the corpus has motif labels (`Zebu` 44, `Elep` 51, `Buff` 15, `Rhin` 23, `Tigr` 13).
- **Test.**
  - Look for any sign that is a picture of the motif animal (as G706 is for the elephant).
  - Check its rate on that motif's seals against other seals. The project's earlier finding that text is independent of motif predicts no excess.
  - An excess for one animal would mark a seal type where the text names the animal or its emblem-group.
- **Data needed.**
  - Possehl 1996a's list of about 40 "Indian" words, giving spellings, texts and dates.
  - Picture-sign identifications for zebu, buffalo and rhinoceros from Fuls's or Parpola's sign list.

**Not ranked, but worth one line each.**
- **Peacock.** Sumerian *ḫa-ia* is onomatopoeic, so it is not a loan.
- **lāṅgala.** There is no plough sign.
- **gišimmar / śimbala.** The tree is not pictured.
- **Meluhha / \*m(e)lu-.** This is a possible ethnonym in Gulf-seal texts. A test would compare the shared initial sign groups on the about 27 Laursen Gulf seals, but it needs a sound value.
