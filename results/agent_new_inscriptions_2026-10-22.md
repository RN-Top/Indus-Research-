# Sources of Indus inscriptions plausibly missing from ICIT (searched 2026-10-22)

Scope: sources with sign-by-sign readable images (photos or drawings) of Indus texts that might be absent from the user's ICIT export (5,680 objects).
Legend: **[V]** verified from a fetched page. **[U]** unverified or inferred. Nothing below is invented. Where a number is unknown, it says so.

## 0. Key finding first: what ICIT already covers

The table of contents of Fuls, *Corpus of Indus Inscriptions* (2022; built from the ICIT database, version 2.8, May 2022) lists **73 site entries** **[V]** (academia.edu/83046175). They include almost every site named in the brief:

Alamgirpur, Allahdino, Altyn Depe, Amri, Bakkar Buthi, Balakot, Banawali, Baror, **Bhirrana**, Chandigarh, Chanhujo-daro, Daimabad, Derawar Ther, Desalpur, **Dholavira**, Failaka, **Farmana**, **Ganweriwala**, Gharo Bhiro, Girsu, **Gola Dhoro (Bagasra)**, Gonur Depe, Guddal A, Gumla, Hajar, Harappa, Hissam-dheri, Hulas, Janabiyah, Jhukar, Kalibangan, **Kanmer**, **Karanpura**, Karzakan, **Khirsara**, Kish, Kot-Diji, **Lakhanjo-daro**, Lohumjo-daro, Lothal, Luristan, Miri Qalat, Mohenjo-daro, Naru-Waro-dharo, Nausharo, Nindowari-damb, Nippur, Nuhato, Pabumath, Pirak, Qala'at al-Bahrain, **Ra's al-Junayz**, Rahman-dheri, Rajanpur, **Rakhigarhi**, Rappwala Ther, Rodji, Rupar, **Saar**, **Salut**, **Shikarpur**, Shortughai, Sibri, Surkotada, Susa, Tarkhanewala-dera, Tell Umma, Tello, Tepe Yahya, **Tigrana**, Unknown, Ur, Wattoowala.

The book's 2022 totals are 4,660 inscribed artefacts and 5,644 texts, plus an "Extended Corpus" (ECIT) of 888 artefacts with iconography only **[V]**. 4,660 + 888 = 5,548, which is close to the user's 5,680. So the export is most likely ICIT plus ECIT with some later additions **[U, inference]**.

What this means:
- Being "site absent from ICIT" is rare. The real gaps are **(a) incomplete coverage of sites that ICIT does list** (big excavation reports whose full catalogues ICIT may only partly contain) and **(b) finds published after about mid-2022**.
- **Check this before using any source below:** count the export's objects per site (Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Farmana, Kanmer, Khirsara, Shikarpur, Gola Dhoro, Bhirrana, Karanpura, Chanhujo-daro) and compare each count with the report's count.
- **Fastest route:** the user is in contact with Fuls and Wells. Ask them directly which accession numbers from Bisht 2015 (Dholavira) and the Rakhigarhi reports are in ICIT.

CISI coverage, for reference **[V]**:
- Vol. 1 (1987): collections in India.
- Vol. 2 (1991): collections in Pakistan.
- Vol. 3.1 (2010): Mohenjo-daro and Harappa supplement, including HARP material (3,761 b/w photographs).
- Vol. 3.2 (released Jan 2020): Shahr-i Sokhta, Mundigak, Mehrgarh, Nausharo, Sibri, Dauda-damb, Chanhudaro, Ahar, Balathal, Gilund, Kalibangan, Rojdi.
- Vol. 3.3 (2022/23): Indo-Iranian borderlands. Its reviewer says it contains "hardly any Indus-type seals".
- Vol. 3.4: no publication found **[U]**.
- **No CISI volume covers Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Kanmer, Farmana, Bhirrana, Khirsara, Shikarpur or Gola Dhoro.** ICIT's entries for those sites must come from excavation reports and papers, so they may be incomplete. That is the main opening.

## Ranked sources (by estimated genuinely new, readable texts)

### 1. Dholavira: R.S. Bisht, *Excavations at Dholavira 1989–2005* (ASI, 2015; about 940 pp.)
- **Contents [V, from excerpt]:**
  - Section 8.2.3 (pp. 260–307) catalogues **225 seals**, "most of them with photographs". Some broken seals are not illustrated, e.g. no. 206.
  - Fig. 8.23 is a motif pie chart covering 136 seals. Fig. 11.1 is a composite of 176 seals.
  - Sealings: Figs. 8.28–8.30. Terracotta tablet Acc. No. 8099: Figs. 8.31–8.32.
  - Other inscriptions: the ten-sign signboard (Figs. 8.2–8.7, pp. 228–231); a four-sign stone block from the Bailey (Figs. 8.8–8.9); an incised terracotta cake (Fig. 8.10); a storage jar (Fig. 7.46); a three-sign slab fragment and an inscribed potsherd (no figure numbers given in the excerpt).
  - Catalogue entries state sign counts, e.g. "159 Black steatite seal. 3 signs", "174 … 2 signs", "170 … Inscription: 3 strokes".
- **Access:**
  - Excerpt PDF: https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/IndOc-Gulf/Dholavira-Bisht2015.pdf
  - Full text on Scribd: https://www.scribd.com/document/262316120/ (blocked by JS challenge here).
  - It is an ASI publication: copyright ASI, research and reading use.
  - Image quality in the scans is unverified. Many photos in ASI reports are small halftones, so sign-level legibility needs checking plate by plate **[U]**.
- **Why plausibly missing:** published 2015, and Dholavira is in no CISI volume. ICIT lists Dholavira, but its count is unknown to me **[U]**.
- **Estimate:** if the export has far fewer than about 150 Dholavira texts, this is **the single largest source: roughly 50–150 new readable texts [U]**. If ICIT already ingested Bisht 2015, it yields about 0.

### 2. Chanhu-daro new excavations, 2015–2022 (Italian–Pakistani mission: Vidale, Mallah et al.)
- **Publications [V titles]:**
  - "Resuming excavations at Chanhu-daro, Sindh: First results of the 2015–2017 field-seasons" (HAL halshs-02975449).
  - "Chanhu-daro, field-seasons 2015–2022. Contextual data and stratigraphy" (HAL halshs-04365857).
  - Both are on HAL (open archive), but HAL blocked automated access here. **Whether they include inscribed seals or sealings, and how many, is unverified.**
- **Why plausibly missing:** fieldwork after 2014. CISI 3.2's Chanhudaro section covers old (Mackay 1935–36) material.
- **Estimate:** 0–20 **[U]**. Worth opening the HAL PDFs manually.

### 3. Rakhigarhi (three separate excavation campaigns)
- **(a) A. Nath, *Excavations at Rakigarhi 1997–98 to 1999–2000*** (ASI draft report, dated 31.12.2014; about 400 pp.).
  - Free download at https://www.harappa.com/content/excavations-rakigarhi-1997-98-1999-2000 **[V]**; also on academia.edu/24830219.
  - It has a "Graffiti" chapter (Ch. 8, p. 205) **[V]**. The excavation's seal count was not visible in the text I could read **[U]**.
  - It notes that earlier Rakhigarhi seals were already in Joshi & Parpola (CISI 1), plus two published by Phadke and one in the Jhajjar Museum **[V]**.
- **(b) Shinde (Deccan College), 2011–2016.**
  - No formal final report with a seal catalogue was found **[U]**.
  - Seal images circulate only through secondary sources, e.g. Shinde's "Harappan Civilization: Current Perspective and its Contribution" (two one-horned-bull seals) and a seal with a crocodile and "five Harappan letters" mentioned in a 2025 survey paper (academia.edu/164736457) **[V that these are mentioned; images and provenance not verified]**.
  - Kalyanaraman's academia pages (e.g. academia.edu/25798943, "Rakhigarhi inscriptions (21)") reproduce photos but are not primary sources. Use them only to locate originals.
- **(c) ASI 2021–2024 (Mounds 1, 3 and 7; S.K. Manjul and V.K. Gupta).**
  - V.K. Gupta, "A Note on Harappan Culture … First Season's Excavation at Rakhigarhi Mound No. 3 (2021–22)", *Vestigia Indica* (2025/26). The abstract reports a steatite seal **[V]**.
  - Press (May 2022) reports "seals" from three of seven mounds, with no count **[V]**.
- **Why plausibly missing:** (b) and (c) date from after 2014 and after 2022.
- **Estimate:** about 3–15 new readable texts in total **[U]**.

### 4. Bhirrana surface seal (Uesugi, Jamison, Dangi & Nakayama 2016)
- *Heritage: Journal of Multidisciplinary Studies in Archaeology* 4: 1–17. Open PDF: https://www.heritageuniversityofkerala.com/JournalPDF/Volume4/1.pdf **[V]**
- One right-facing unicorn seal from surface collection with **3 legible signs**, shown as a photo plus a 3D scan of its impression (Fig. 2) **[V]**.
- Also redrawn: three excavated Bhirrana seals (Fig. 3, from Rao et al. 2005; probably already in ICIT) and comparison seals from Farmana and Bagasra.
- **Estimate:** 1 new text (the surface seal) **[U as to ICIT absence]**.

### 5. Farmana: Shinde, Osada, Uesugi & Kumar, *A Report on Excavations at Farmana 2007–08* (Indus Project Occasional Paper 6, RIHN Kyoto, 2008); final monograph 2011
- Open PDF: https://nidanaheights.com/images/Library/ranbirbooks/op6.pdf **[V]**
- Contents: steatite seals (Figs. 106–107), a clay sealing (Fig. 108), and a clay tablet with a seal impression (Fig. 109). Text mentions a buffalo seal with signs, a humped-bull seal with signs, a unicorn-stamped amulet, and possibly inscribed sherds **[V]**.
- Farmana is an ICIT site, so these are probably in ICIT.
- **Estimate:** 0–5 new **[U]**. Check the export.

### 6. Gola Dhoro (Bagasra): Jamison, Sharma, Ajithprasad, Krishnan, Bhan & Sonawane, "Inscribed Unicorn Seals from Bagasra, Gujarat"
- 7 inscribed unicorn seals with photographs **[V]**: https://www.harappa.com/content/inscribed-unicorn-seals-bagasra-gujarat-comparative-analysis-morphology-carving-styles-and (download via academia.edu/36047001).
- Gola Dhoro is an ICIT site, so these are probably already included.
- **Estimate:** 0–7 **[U]**. The same applies to Shikarpur (Bhan & Ajithprasad 2008+; harappa.com video "Harappan seals and sealings from Bagasra and Shikarpur").

### 7. Kanmer and Khirsara
- **Kanmer:** Kharakwal et al., "Harappan Script Material from Kanmer" (academia.edu/4891402; fetch failed), and the three "passes" (Kharakwal et al. 2012, cited by Parpola 2018) **[V citation]**. Kanmer is an ICIT site.
- **Khirsara** (ASI, J. Nath, 2009–2013): Wikipedia mentions one Indus-script tablet, and seals displayed at the Kutch Museum. No published seal catalogue was found **[V/U]**. Khirsara is an ICIT site.
- **Estimate:** 0–5 **[U]**.

### 8. CISI vol. 3.3, *Indo-Iranian Borderlands* (Parpola & Koskikallio, Finnish Academy, 2022/23; €220)
- Large photographic volume of mostly pre-Indus geometric seals. Its reviewer says it has "hardly any Indus-type seals" **[V]**.
- ICIT already lists several borderland sites (Miri Qalat, Sibri, Rahman-dheri, Gumla, Nindowari, Pirak).
- **Estimate:** 0–10 Indus-script texts **[U]**. Mostly not script.

## Bottom line
**There is no large published body of post-ICIT Indus texts.** Realistic yield:
- Dholavira (Bisht 2015) is the only source that could add more than about 50 texts, and only if ICIT under-covers it.
- Everything else together is probably about 10–40 texts.

If the Dholavira gap turns out small, the sealed predictions will lack power on truly new data. Two fallbacks:
- (a) Use the Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Chanhu-daro and Bhirrana additions as a small confirmatory set.
- (b) Ask Fuls or Wells for ICIT objects added after the user's export date.
