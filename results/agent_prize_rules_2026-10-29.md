# Tamil Nadu US$1 million Indus script prize: terms, status, and other funding

Research date: 2026-10-29. Each fact is marked:
- **[V]**: verified on a fetched page (URL and quote given). Quotes come from the WebFetch tool's extraction, which may paraphrase slightly. Where the tool said a quote was verbatim, that is noted.
- **[U]**: unverified (search-result title or snippet only, inference, or a page I could not fetch).

## Bottom line

No published official rules exist for the prize. I found no rules document, no named judging committee, no submission address or portal, no deadline and no evaluation criteria in any fetched source, and searches of Tamil Nadu government sites turned up no official press release text. Everything public comes from news reports of one sentence in the Chief Minister's speech on 5 January 2025. As of late September 2026, press reports describe the prize as **unclaimed**. The government that announced it (DMK, M.K. Stalin) left office in May 2026, and Vijay (TVK) is now Chief Minister. I found no statement from the new government on whether the prize still stands.

Scroll.in, dated 6 Jan 2025: the announcement came at "a centenary event celebrating the discovery of the civilisation by English archaeologist John Marshall." https://scroll.in/latest/1077596/tamil-nadu-announces-1-million-prize-for-deciphering-indus-valley-civilisation-script
- **[V]** Date: Sunday, 5 January 2025, at a three-day international conference in Chennai. Business Standard: remarks made "at a three-day international conference on Sunday, January 5, 2025." https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/cm-stalin-announces-1-million-prize-for-decoding-indus-valley-script-125010500330_1.html
- **[V]** Deccan Herald (ETB Sivapriyan, 05 Jan 2025, 16:29 IST): "I announce a cash prize of $1 million to individuals or organisations that decipher the script". The extraction tool says this is verbatim but may be shortened. The same report says the prize goes to those whose work is accepted by archaeological experts. https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Ftamil-nadu%2Fcm-stalin-announces-1-million-prize-for-decoding-indus-valley-script-3342165

### Who is eligible
- **[V]** "individuals or organisations" (ThePrint/PTI, Deccan Herald, above). Tamil: "நபர்கள் அல்லது அமைப்புக்கு", meaning "to persons or organisations" (TNPSC Thervupettagam).
- **[V]** Scroll paraphrases this as "any expert or organisation that succeeds in deciphering the script".
- **[V]** Smithsonian says it is open to "anyone—scholar or amateur—who can crack the code." This is the magazine's gloss, not an official term. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/officials-are-offering-1-million-to-anyone-who-can-decode-this-ancient-script-180985922/
- **[U]** Foreigners and people without degrees: **no source says either way.** No fetched source mentions nationality, degree, or affiliation restrictions. The announced wording ("individuals or organisations") contains none. Whether any would be added at payout is unknown.

### What counts as "deciphering"
- **[V]** The only stated test is acceptance by archaeological experts. Tamil: "தொல்லியல் அறிஞர்கள் ஏற்கும்படி", meaning "in a way that archaeological scholars accept" (TNPSC Thervupettagam). English reports say "to the satisfaction of archaeological experts" (Business Standard 4 Feb 2025 roundup: https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/tamil-nadu-cm-announces-1-million-for-deciphering-indus-valley-script-nc-125020400475_1.html; Smithsonian).
- **[V]** No definition was given. Business Standard (Feb 2025): "The article names no judges, panel, or criteria" (the extraction tool's summary).
- **[V]** A commentator raised the definition problem, for context: Languagehat, comment by "Ryan" on 18 Jan 2025: "An interesting question is what threshold must be crossed to consider the code 'cracked'." https://languagehat.com/a-million-for-harappan/

### Who judges, how to submit, deadlines
- **[V]** Not specified in any fetched source: Scroll, Smithsonian, Business Standard (both articles), ThePrint, DT Next, ETV Bharat, Euronews and IAS Express. For example, DT Next: "No judges, panel, or expert reviewers are named"; the article doesn't describe "submission, criteria, judging, or deadlines" (extraction tool's summary). https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/tamilnadu/cm-stalin-announces-one-million-usd-prize-to-unravel-indus-valley-civilization-mystery-818092

### Announced alongside the prize (same speech)
- **[V]** Rs 2 crore for a research chair named after Iravatham Mahadevan, run with the Indus Research Centre at Roja Muthiah Research Library. There are also annual awards for two scholars in epigraphy and numismatics (TNPSC Thervupettagam; Business Standard; ETV Bharat https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/tn-cm-stalin-announces-prize-for-decoding-indus-valley-script-enn25010503182).
- **[V]** The TN Archaeology Department study "Indus Signs and Graffiti Marks of Tamil Nadu" was released at the event (IAS Express, 10 Jan 2025: https://www.iasexpress.net/the-indus-script-decoded-tamil-nadus-1-million-challenge-explained/).

- **[U]** Whether the Vijay government has kept, changed, or quietly dropped the prize: **no information found** in English or Tamil searches.
- **[U]** Claimed winners or formal submissions: none found. Public claimants (Yajnadevam, who argues for Sanskrit; Bahata Ansumali Mukhopadhyay, who argues for a non-phonetic, commercial script) are covered in the press. I found no report of either being evaluated for the prize.

### ASI seminar: "Decipherment of Indus Script: Current Status and Way Forward"
- **[V]** ThePrint, 20 Jun 2025: organised by the Archaeological Survey of India with the Ministry of Culture, 20–22 August 2025, at Pandit Deendayal Upadhyaya Institute of Archaeology, Greater Noida. It was open to "Scholars, researchers, and any individual who, by their own volition" are pursuing decipherment. Presentations could be given offline or online, and "It is expected that the papers will be submitted prior to the date of the seminar." https://theprint.in/feature/asi-calling-researchers-to-crack-indus-valley-script/2664475/

These could not be fetched (robots.txt blocks automated fetching), so their contents are unverified. Open them in a browser.

## Practical notes (inference, [U])
- With no rules, committee or portal, there is currently **no defined way to submit** for the prize. The only route visible in sources is writing to the Tamil Nadu Archaeology Department or the Indus Research Centre (RMRL). This is my inference, not an official instruction.
- Since the test is acceptance by archaeological experts, peer-reviewed publication and engagement with specialists such as the IRC and Mahadevan Chair are the realistic path. A direct claim is not.
- Confirm with the current (TVK) government whether the prize still stands before investing effort in a claim.
