# Claimed continuities between Indus signs and later systems: an evidence audit

Prepared for the Indus-Research- project (Erin Rae Toppe). Compiled 2026-10-22.

Grades: **A** demonstrated, **B** suggestive, **C** weak, **D** fringe.
The grade applies to the *continuity or meaning claim*, not to the fact that an object exists.
Some details come only from news reports or reviews because the primary text could not be fetched. Those cases are flagged.
Pages that could not be fetched this session: tngraffiti.in; the Rajan & Sivanantham PDF on tamildigitallibrary.in; the RMRL PDFs of Mahadevan 2001 and 2007; the journal.fi copy of Studia Orientalia (bot wall); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimabad.

---

## Bottom line

- No object is known on which an Indus sign sequence stands next to a readable text giving the same message, so there is no bilingual. Wikipedia's Indus script article, citing Robinson 2015, says the same.
- Shape overlap between Indus signs and South Indian Iron Age graffiti is real but cheap. Most shared signs are simple geometric marks such as strokes, arrows, X, ladders and fish-like shapes. None of the studies uses a null model, so a high overlap percentage is expected whatever the history.
- The more informative claims are at the level of **sequences** (Mahadevan's pairs and triplets). They are anecdotal and cherry-picked, but they *can be tested* statistically against an Indus corpus.
- Linear Elamite (Desset et al. 2022) is now largely readable. Fuls (2024), the specialist comparison, concludes that it and Indus are probably unrelated. **No Linear Elamite phonetic value can responsibly be transferred to Indus.**
- The only real "partial bilingual" situations are (a) Tamil sherds carrying both graffiti marks and Tamil-Brahmi, and (b) Indus-style seals from the Gulf and Mesopotamia in a cuneiform-literate setting. Neither gives a value for an Indus sign today.

## 1. South Indian megalithic / Iron Age graffiti

### 1a. B. B. Lal, "From the Megalithic to the Harappa: tracing back the graffiti on the pottery", *Ancient India* 16 (1960; some references give 1962)
- **Claim:** Most megalithic graffiti signs have Harappan counterparts, so there was cultural continuity.
- **Evidence:** A photographic catalogue of graffiti-marked megalithic pottery compared by eye with Indus signs.
- **Statistics:** Secondary sources disagree. Wikipedia's "Megalithic graffiti symbols" gives **89%**. The "Indus script" article, citing Ray 2006 pp. 21–22, gives **47 of 61 signs** (= 77%). The primary text was not checked. There was no control comparison and no frequency weighting.
- **Criticism:** Simple shapes recur independently. The gap is more than 1,000 years, with no intervening chain.
- **URLs:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalithic_graffiti_symbols ; https://keibi.uni-tuebingen.de/Record/KEI00117012
- **Grade: C**

### 1b. K. Rajan & R. Sivanantham, *Indus Signs and Graffiti Marks of Tamil Nadu: A Morphological Study* (Tamil Nadu State Dept. of Archaeology; released January 2025 at the Indus centenary)
- **Claim:** "Nearly 90%" (lead) or "more than 90%" (body) of graffiti marks have Indus parallels. The two figures are not reconciled in the PTI report. Harappa.com's review adds that **60% of the signs** "found their parallels in the Indus script". The authors present possible contact or contemporaneity as a hypothesis and say "more evidence is needed" (PTI).
- **Data:** More than 15,000 graffiti-bearing sherds from about 140 sites, including Keeladi, Thulukarpatti, Arikamedu, Uraiyur, Korkai, Alangulam, Adichanallur, Kodumanal and Kilnamandi. The marks are classified into **42 base signs, 544 variants and 1,521 composite forms**. The searchable database is https://tngraffiti.in, built by RMRL with the TN Archaeology Department.
- **Method:** Morphological (visual) classification, then comparison with images of Indus seals and graffiti. Reported sources show no null model, no frequency comparison, no sequence analysis, no independent coders, and no peer-reviewed article.
- **Statistical rigor:** Low. "90% of sherds" is dominated by a few very common simple marks. The headline is a sherd-weighted figure, not a sign-type figure, and the two are different numbers. No baseline (for example random geometric marks, or Deccan/Sri Lankan/Mediterranean potters' marks) was tested.
- **Criticism:**
  - Harappa.com's own review flags the ~1,000-year gap, independent invention of simple shapes, and the difficulty of comparing seals with graffiti.
  - Language Hat commenters (Jan 2025) note that the marks are "few and simple; they could be anything" (D. Marjanović), that there was no publication, that the sherds show mostly isolated signs rather than repeating sequences, and that a pre-Keeladi survey of Deccan marks looked more like the Keeladi marks than the Harappan ones (commenter "Y").
  - The claims arrived with a political frame: the TN CM's $1M decipherment prize was announced at the same release.
  - Harry Falk's broader warning (via the Wikipedia Tamil-Brahmi article) is that megalithic graffiti are non-linguistic and get misread.
- **Related chronology claim:** TN Archaeology's "Antiquity of Iron" (Jan 2025) puts iron at Sivagalai and other sites at 2953–3345 BCE. If true it would shrink the gap, but it is not peer reviewed and is contested. It is reported here only via news (Tamil Guardian, 5 Mar 2025).
- **URLs:** https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/01/06/mes7-tn-excavations-indus-valley.html ; https://www.harappa.com/content/indus-signs-and-graffiti-marks-tamil-nadu ; https://www.harappa.com/blog/indus-concordance-and-tamil-potsherds-now-online ; https://languagehat.com/a-million-for-harappan/ ; https://www.tamilguardian.com/node/952553
- **Grade: C.** The overlap is real at the level of shapes, but nothing shows it is non-random. The database itself is valuable and testable.

### 1c. Iravatham Mahadevan: specific sign *sequences* on megalithic and early historic pottery
- **Sanur (near Tindivanam):** Mahadevan, "Indus-like symbols on megalithic pottery: new evidence", 2001 (RMRL list; reportedly in *Studia Orientalia* 94, the Parpola Festschrift, but the venue was not verified because of the bot wall). Not read directly.
- **Sulur dish:** A terracotta dish (British Museum, c. 100 BCE) whose inscription he compared with a three-sided tablet from Harappa: "A megalithic pottery inscription and a Harappa tablet: a case of extraordinary resemblance", *Journal of Tamil Studies* 71 (2007). He argued that such resemblance is "possible only if the south Indian megalithic script is related to the Indus script". The sign IDs were not retrievable (PDF blocked).
- **Sembiyankandiyur and Melaperumpallam (2008):** On three pots an arrow-like sign is "always incised twice and together". Mahadevan compared this with paired arrows on Mohenjo-daro seals and conceded that the form differs slightly.
- **Phu Khao Thong, Thailand (2010):** A sherd with two non-Brahmi signs: an "N"-like sign (≈ Mahadevan 1977 nos. 47/48) followed by a diamond (≈ nos. 261/373). Mahadevan says the same pair occurs in Harappa texts **4589 and 5265**. The site also yielded Tamil-Brahmi of about the 2nd century CE.
- **Statistical rigor:** None. These are hand-picked hits, with no estimate of how often a 2-sign match would occur by chance given Indus sign frequencies.
- **Criticism:** The same as 1a and 1b, plus selection bias. A 2-sign match between common simple signs in a corpus of thousands of texts is not surprising.
- **URLs:** https://orientalthane.com/archaeology/news_2008_05_12.htm ; https://orientalthane.com/archaeology/news_2010_04_09.htm ; https://www.harappa.com/content/arrow/megalithic-inscription.html
- **Grade: C**, but this is the most *testable* family of claims. See "Testable next steps".

### 1d. Sembiyankandiyur stone celt (2006)
- **Claim:** A polished Neolithic celt carries four Indus-type signs (dated c. 1500 BCE per reports). Mahadevan took it to show that Neolithic Tamil Nadu and the Indus shared a language, "which can only be Dravidian".
- **Evidence:** A schoolteacher (V. Shanmuganathan) found it, not a stratified excavation. The engraving date is not independently established, and the celt's date does not date the incision.
- **URLs:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sembiyankandiyur ; https://www.harappa.com/content/arrow/stone_celt_indus_signs.html
- **Grade: C/D.** The linguistic inference does not follow.

### 1e. Thelunganur (Salem district), reported October 2026
- **Claim:** One intact black-and-red ware bowl has **five graffiti signs** written left to right, reported as the longest sequence in Tamil Nadu. A second bowl has **the same five signs in a different order**. The sites (with Mangadu) are given as c. 1600–1200 BCE. The press presents it as a "possible" writing system or proto-Tamil-Brahmi.
- **Evidence:** News and exam-prep summaries only. No named authors, no publication, and no published dating method for the bowls.
- **Why it matters:** The same sign set in a different order is the kind of thing that distinguishes ownership-mark sets from linear writing. If the order is free, that argues *against* linear writing.
- **URLs:** https://c4scourses.in/national-affairs/3000-year-old-writing-system-possibly-found-at-thelunganur/ ; https://www.tnpscthervupettagam.com//currentaffairs-detail/thelunganur-archaeological-discovery
- **Grade: C (pending publication)**

### 1f. Graffiti → Tamil-Brahmi stratigraphy and co-occurrence (Rajan)
- **Claim (Rajan 2008, via Wikipedia):** At Kodumanal and similar sites, the lowest levels have symbols only, the middle levels have symbols mixed with Brahmi, and the top levels have Brahmi only. Symbols leave grave contexts around 300 CE. Rajan sees the graffiti as a precursor stage to Tamil-Brahmi.
- **Marungur sherd (Cuddalore district, reported 2010):** A sherd with Tamil-Brahmi "a-m" plus **two graffiti marks** that Rajan says resemble Indus signs. It is dated palaeographically to the 1st century BCE. This is the closest thing to a partial "bilingual" in this survey, but it is co-occurrence, not translation, and the sherd is broken.
- **Criticism:** Falk argues that some "earliest Tamil-Brahmi" readings are misinterpreted graffiti, and the early (5th–6th c. BCE) Porunthal and Keeladi dates are disputed (Falk, Shulman). The Wire (Oct 2019) reported that experts questioned the Keeladi script dates.
- **URLs:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil-Brahmi ; https://orientalthane.com/archaeology/news_2010_03_06_C.htm ; https://thewire.in/history/keeladi-settlement-tamil-nadu-department-of-archaeology-tamil-brahmi-script-indus-valley-civilisation
- **Grade:** **B** that graffiti and Tamil-Brahmi overlap in time and sometimes share sherds. **C** for any Indus value carried through.

## 2. Indus → Brahmi, and Late Harappan / Chalcolithic survivals

### 2a. G. R. Hunter, *The Script of Harappa and Mohenjodaro and its Connection with Other Scripts* (1934)
- **Claim:** Brahmi derives from Indus. Hunter judged the match "considerably higher" than Aramaic's, and also linked Indus to South Arabian and Phoenician.
- **Evidence:** Visual comparison of signs.
- **Rigor:** None by modern standards.
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script
- **Grade: C**

### 2b. Subhash Kak, *Cryptologia* 12(3) (1988): 129–143
- **Claim:** The ten most frequent Indus signs and ligatures resemble frequent Brahmi letters, and four of the top five signs look alike. Chance is "improbable".
- **Criticism:** Witzel (Indology list, July 2000) says the method leaves enough freedom to reach any reading, and that his own frequency tables do not resemble Kak's. The match criteria were subjective.
- **URL:** https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2000-July/021871.html
- **Grade: C/D**

### 2c. S. R. Rao (1982 and later)
- **Claim:** Indus is an Indo-Aryan, partly alphabetic script ancestral to later Indian and Semitic alphabets.
- **Criticism:** Mitchiner called it "greatly subjective and unconvincing".
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikaripura_Ranganatha_Rao
- **Grade: D**

### 2d. D. P. Sharma (2012)
- **Claim:** A "proto-Brahmi" palm-leaf manuscript from a Harappan site in Afghanistan.
- **Evidence:** A press claim only, with no image, date or publication.
- **URL:** https://varnam.org/2012/11/did-harappans-write-in-proto-brahmi/
- **Grade: D**

### 2e. C. L. Fábri (1935)
- **Claim:** Symbols on punch-marked coins are Indus survivals.
- **Grade: C/D**, with the same shape-overlap problem.

### Consensus on Brahmi
- Salomon (*Indian Epigraphy* 1998, pp. 20–21) says graphic similarity cannot be evaluated without Indus phonetic values, and calls the compounding and diacritic patterns "intriguing" but premature.
- Scharfe (2002) calls such attempts "total failures".
- Falk (2018) argues that Brahmi was a deliberate Ashokan-era creation.
- Mahadevan himself conceded that "a thousand years still separates the two".
- Majority view: Brahmi has Semitic (Aramaic) influence, though this is still debated.
- **Grade for any Indus → Brahmi value transfer: C at best.**

### 2f. Late Harappan script outside the core
- **Daimabad (Maharashtra):** S. A. Sali's 1978–79 excavation found **two terracotta button seals and three sherds with Indus signs**, Late Harappan (Period II). Wikipedia's Indus script article (citing Sali 1986, pp. 504–505) dates them c. 2200–1600 BCE.
  - Sources: https://theprint.in/opinion/daimabad-home-to-skilled-agriculturalists-before-harappans-cultural-influence/2382933/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script
  - **Grade A** that the script was used in the Late Harappan Deccan. It is no anchor, because no readable script is present.
- **Rangpur (Gujarat):** Kenoyer (2006) says the script ceased at Harappa c. 1900 BCE but lingered elsewhere, for example at Rangpur, mainly as pottery graffiti. Jhukar-phase seals lack the script.
  - **Grade B/A**, a decline into graffiti.
- **Bet Dwarka:** Late Harappan seal and an inscribed jar (Rao's 1980s marine excavations). The details of the inscription, and of Rao's reading, could not be verified from accessible sources.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bet_Dwarka
  - **A** that the objects exist (as reported). **D** for any reading.
- **Kenoyer, pre-Indus continuity backward:** Kenoyer argues that Indus signs grew out of earlier pottery graffiti (Kalibangan, Balakot, Kot Diji phase at Harappa). Mahadevan sign 342 may appear post-firing on a Kot Dijian sherd. Graffiti continued alongside the script.
  - Source: https://www.harappa.com/content/origin-and-development-indus-script-insights-harappa-and-other-sites
  - **B.** Methodological point: graffiti marks and script signs co-existed as *different* systems with partly shared shapes. That weakens the inference "graffiti shape = script sign with the same value", including for Tamil Nadu.
- **Sanauli:** No report of Indus-script inscriptions was found in this search. Treat any such claim as unsupported until it is cited.
- **Kudikadu:** No usable source was found this session.

## 3. Proto-Elamite / Linear Elamite

### 3a. Hunter (1932) and later authors on Proto-Elamite
- **Claim:** Proto-Elamite and Indus resemble each other too closely for coincidence.
- **Evidence:** About 35 Proto-Elamite signs are called "comparable" to Indus signs (Zvelebil 1990, pp. 85–86, 96). Langdon disputed this.
- **Consensus:** The two scripts are not closely related, though there may be stimulus diffusion (Possehl 2002, p. 136).
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script
- **Grade: C**

### 3b. Andreas Fuls, "Comparison of Linear Elamite and Indus Writing Systems", *Iranian Journal of Archaeological Studies* 14(1) (June 2024): 3–16, DOI 10.22111/ijas.2024.48980.1305
- **Conclusion (abstract):** The two systems are "likely not directly related". The *idea* of writing may have spread between the regions from about 2200 BCE. They share **methods of sign modification and reading direction** but **differ considerably in writing type and degree of phonetization**.
- **Shared signs:** The abstract names no shared signs and transfers no values.
- **URLs:** https://ijas.usb.ac.ir/article_8463.html ; https://doaj.org/article/b949e0889334492fbbeb122fc12454e8
- **Grade:** For "Indus and Linear Elamite are related", **D/C**. Fuls's own negative conclusion is the credible position.
- **Useful takeaway:** Fuls points to *structural* parallels (how signs are modified, reading direction), not to sign values. Those parallels could be used as typological priors, for example that diacritic-like modifications behave as grammatical or phonetic modifiers.

### 3c. F. Desset, K. Tabibzadeh, M. Kervran, G. P. Basello, G. Marchesi, "The Decipherment of Linear Elamite Writing", *ZA* 112 (2022): 11–60, DOI 10.1515/za-2022-0003
- **Method:**
  - Anchored by Susa monuments of Puzur-Sushinak with Akkadian cuneiform.
  - Uses the names Shilhaha, Eparti and Napirisha on silver beakers.
  - Draws on Elamite digraphia, where the same Elamite text appears in both scripts.
- **Result:** A phonetic script (vowels, consonants, CV). Wikipedia gives 348 glyphs, about 80–110 graphemes, of which **72 are phonographic**.
- **Criticism:**
  - Peers withhold judgment until full translations appear (Smithsonian). Krebernik finds it "mostly convincing".
  - **Mäder:** about 70% of signs are phonographic and the rest logograms, so it is not a pure syllabary.
  - **Dahl (2009, 2022):** few practitioners, no standard signary, possible pseudo-glyphs, and an archaizing borrowing from Proto-Elamite rather than evolution from it.
  - **Provenance:** The silver beakers are unprovenanced private-collection material (Mahboubian). Smithsonian reports metallurgy indicating they are ancient, but the findspot is unconfirmed.
- **URLs:** https://anetoday.org/desset-irans-linear-elamite-deciphered/ ; https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=56019 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_Elamite ; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/have-scholars-finally-deciphered-a-mysterious-ancient-script-180980497/
- **Grade: B+** as a decipherment of Linear Elamite.

### Can a Linear Elamite value transfer to Indus?
**No.**
- No genetic relationship between the two scripts has been shown, and the best comparative study argues against one.
- Linear Elamite is mostly phonographic, while Indus is very probably logo-syllabic or logographic-heavy.
- The Elamite language has no demonstrated relationship to the Indus language. Language Log commenters note that Elamo-Dravidian is unsupported.
- Any shared shapes would be graphic coincidences. Assigning Elamite sound values to them would be unanchored guessing.
- **Grade for transfer: D.**

## Testable next steps for the project

1. **Null model for sequence matches (Mahadevan-type claims).** Using the project's Indus corpus frequencies, compute the expected number of corpus texts containing a given ordered 2- or 3-sign pair. Then ask whether the reported matches (Phu Khao Thong ≈ 47/48 + 261/373; Sulur dish vs Harappa tablet; paired arrows) are rarer than chance. This is cheap and decisive for 1c.
2. **Baseline for the "90%" figure.** Compare the 42 tngraffiti.in base signs against (a) Indus signs and (b) a control inventory of unrelated potters' marks, for example Deccan Chalcolithic, Egyptian pot marks or Aegean marks. If the overlap with the control is similar, the 90% figure carries no signal.
3. **Gulf-seal sequences** are the best *in-period* lead. If Indus-sign sequences on Dilmun seals could be matched to Dilmun personal names known from cuneiform (Amorite/Akkadian/local), that would anchor sign values. No such matching is published.
4. **Graffiti–Tamil-Brahmi co-occurrence table.** Extract every sherd with both graffiti and Tamil-Brahmi (Kodumanal, Marungur, Keeladi, Porunthal) from tngraffiti.in. Test whether specific marks correlate with specific Brahmi names or words. This would anchor the *graffiti*. Carrying the result back to Indus still depends on step 1 or 2 succeeding.
