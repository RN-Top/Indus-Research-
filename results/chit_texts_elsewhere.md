# Who or what is named on the unit tablets? (scouting, 1 Nov 2026)

Script `scripts/chit_texts_elsewhere.py`, output `chit_texts_elsewhere_output.txt`. Follows `ration_chit_scouting.md`.

**1. Are unit-tablet texts seal texts, i.e. names of seal-holders?** Not more than other tablet texts.

| Texts | Same as a whole seal text | Found inside some seal text |
|---|---|---|
| Unit-tablet texts (238) | 13% | 24% |
| Other Harappa tablet texts (376, control) | 13% | 30% |

The shared segments are common formula pieces (G100 G740, G176 G740, G220 G520). So the recipients or offices on the chits are **not** an especially seal-holding group, and the texts are typical Harappa tablet texts.

**2. Do general features of the text predict the amount?** No. Against N = 2/3/4, none of these is significant:
- ending in G400: p = 0.56
- containing G740: p = 0.52
- length of 3 or more signs: p = 0.30
- starting with a numeral: p = 0.69
- unit order (V# vs #V): p = 0.29

**Reading.** The amount goes with the **particular text** (`ration_chit_scouting.md`), not with any grammatical feature of it. That is what a list of named recipients or offices with fixed allotments looks like: the amount belongs to the name. A sign-class or formula effect would look different.

Incised tablets carry larger amounts (N = 4 on 35% of them, against 23% of moulded ones). This matches Wells (2015: 58, 65).
