# Copper-tablet phrases turn up on seals: a first look (EXPLORATION)

*20 October 2026. Exploration split only. Quick look; nothing registered.*

**The idea.** The copper tablets tie particular phrases to pictures (`copper_image_test.md`, confirmed on the holdout). If those phrases also occur on seals, the seals can be read in part through that link.

| Copper phrase | Copper image | On seals (exploration) |
|---|---|---|
| **G706 G33** (elephant sign + three long strokes) | elephant | **23 seals.** It is often followed by G520: G706 G33 G520. The seals show unicorns or bulls, not elephants. |
| G503 G615 (from the ×26 manger set) | horned animal with manger | 4 seals, all followed by a closer: G503 G615 (G760) G740 / G154 |
| G240 G845 (tiger set) | tiger | 1 seal |
| Hare (G845 G407 G321), archer (G850 G900), goat (G585 G95) | | not on seals |

**Where G706 sits on seals:** in the middle of the text in 26 of 36 uses (72%, against 37% of all seal signs). It is never at the edges, which are the name-like slots.

**First reading, speculative:**
- The elephant-linked phrase **G706 G33** is a recurring unit across genres.
- On seals it is a fixed **middle phrase**, often closed by the G520 formula, not the owner's name slot.
- That would fit a title or affiliation linked to what the elephant stands for, in the way Parpola reads the copper images as deities. It is also compatible with other readings.

**Next step:** build a full "anchor list" of image-linked phrases from the copper sets. Then map every seal and tablet occurrence by slot (name, middle, before closer) and by context. This is exploration first; a registration would follow only if a clear prediction emerges.
