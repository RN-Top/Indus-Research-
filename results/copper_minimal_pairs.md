**Pattern.**
- **All 4 swaps that change the image are swaps of non-fish signs.** Two of them are bovid or animal-like pairs: G423/G424 (gaur/buffalo) and G705/G706.
- **All 4 swaps that keep the image** are fish-family variants (G233, G220, G231) or other signs in the middle of the text.
- On the copper tablets, some signs name or select the picture, and others, especially the fish forms, vary without changing it.
- That fits the earlier results that inside-marked fish forms behave as modifiers or formula elements, not counted items (preprint 4).

**Limits.**
- Each pair rests on 1–5 tablets. It is suggestive, not a test.
- G423 and G424 are rare (5 and 2 uses in the corpus).
- "Other" and "Comp" are coarse image labels.
- A prior-work check against Parpola's copper-tablet studies (1994; 2008) is needed before any claim of novelty.

**Why it matters for anchors.** Minimal pairs are the classic controlled evidence in decipherment: change one sign, see what changes. Here they pick out a small set of **picture-linked signs**: G706 (elephant), G423 (gaur), G424 (buffalo), G61 (tiger, in this frame). They also separate them from **non-picture signs** (fish variants).
