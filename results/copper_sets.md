# Copper tablets as a text-image key (EXPLORATION)

*20 October 2026. Exploration split only; the 29 holdout copper tablets were not read. Script: `scripts/copper_sets.py`. Numbers: `copper_sets.json`. The lead comes from Parpola 2005, p. 58 (`parpola2005_notes.md`).*

**Data:** 125 copper objects (120 tablets), almost all from Mohenjo-daro. They carry 51 distinct texts, and 23 texts occur two or more times (sets of identical tablets).

## 1. The same text goes with the same image

- Among the 50 tablets with a specific image label, there are 68 pairs that share a text.
- **99% of those pairs show the same image,** against 10% if images were shuffled (p = 0.0002).
- The text–image link on copper tablets is near-deterministic. This confirms Parpola's "semi-bilingual" observation with numbers.

## 2. Formula signs and image-specific signs

- **Shared across many images (the formula):**
  - G740, on 8 images;
  - G407, on 7: the confirmed copper closer;
  - G845, G1, G3, G33, G100, G900, G61.
- **Confined to a single image and present in every text with it:**

| Sign | Image |
|---|---|
| **G321** | hare |
| **G706** | elephant (2 distinct texts, 7 tablets) |
| **G850** | anthropomorphic figure |
| G424 | buffalo (1 tablet only) |

These are **candidates** for the sign that names, or stands for, what is depicted. The other images have several image-only signs but none common to all their texts.

## 3. Cross-check with seals: these signs do NOT name the animal in general

If G706 meant "elephant", it might be expected on elephant seals.
- **It never appears on any of the 23 elephant seals.** Its 36 seal uses are on unicorn seals (18), seals with no animal (13), gaur (3) and zebu (2).
- None of the other candidates tracks its animal on seals either.

**So** the copper-tablet link is specific to that genre. The text may name what the image stands for there (Parpola suggests deities), not the animal word itself. This fits the earlier result that seal animals are not named in seal texts (`animal_signs_nonunicorn.md`).

## Falsifiable predictions to check against photographs

Several sets have no usable image label. From the table above:
1. The 2 tablets reading **G5 G706** (M-514, M-515) should show an **elephant**.
2. The 5 tablets reading the single sign **G777** (M-603, M-604, M-1555 to M-1557) are Parpola's "isolated sign" type. Their image, if they have one, should match another set's.
3. The 26-tablet set **G503 G615 G752 G740**: its image should be one consistent subject, not a mixture.

## What this adds

1. **Parpola's "isolated sign" pattern is confirmed in two sets.** One text appears with an **image** on some tablets and with a **single sign** on others:

   | Text | Image | Single sign in its place |
   |---|---|---|
   | goat text | goat | **G777** |
   | archer text | archer | **G781** |

   The single sign stands in for the image. These are the closest things to a **sign = picture** equation in the corpus. Both signs are horned-jar forms, and both images are horned beings.
2. **Prediction 3 (the 26-tablet set shows one subject): consistent.** Both drawn copies show a bovine.
3. **Our database labels are coarse or inconsistent.**
   - "Othr" hides the bovine, the rhinoceros and the goat.
   - The tiger set carries mixed labels.
   - So the 99% same-image figure above is, if anything, an **under**estimate of how fixed the text–image link is.
4. **Not testable from these plates:** prediction 1 (G5 G706 shows an elephant). No elephant tablet and no G5 G706 tablet is drawn here. Marshall (1931) or CISI vol. 1 would be needed.

## Updated reading

1. **Every set drawn more than once shows the same subject in every drawing.** This holds across both excavation reports:
   - elephant: 1 Marshall drawing, plus 7 database labels;
   - hare: 2;
   - the ×26 set: 5 drawings;
   - goat: 2;
   - archer: 2.
2. **The texts track fine details of the image, not only the species.**
   - The ×26 set (G503 G615 **G752** G740) shows a horned animal with a manger symbol.
   - The goat set shows a similar animal **with a heart mark**.
   - G503 G615 G740, which lacks G752, shows a **spotted composite** animal.

   One sign more or less goes with a different picture. That is what we would expect if the text **describes or names the picture**.
3. **The sign-for-picture substitutions are unchanged:**
   - G777 for the goat image (Mackay XCIII-1 against XCIII-11);
   - G781 for the archer (Mackay XCIII-3 against XCIII-14).
4. **Still untested:** G5 G706 → elephant. Neither excavation report draws that tablet. It needs CISI vol. 1 photographs of M-514 and M-515.

## Prior work: Parpola (2008) already has the sets and the substitutions

*Read 20 October 2026. Erin supplied the PDF.*

**What earlier work already contains:**
- Mackay (1931: 400) saw the link: hare tablets share one inscription, elephant tablets another. Hunter (1934) disputed it.
- Pande (1973) gave 19 inscription types.
- Franke-Vogt (1990) described a "normative relation" between image and text.
- Parpola (1975, 2008) gave a full typology of 46 types:
  - he wrote that the inscription "is enough to predict what is engraved on the other side";
  - he named the image-to-sign pairs "pictorial bilinguals": his types B9/C5 (markhor) and B19/C6 (archer), with others;
  - he treats the reverse sign of C5 and C6 as **one** ligature linking goat and archer. ICIT splits it into G777 and G781.

**What is ours:**
- the measurement against chance;
- the set-by-set check of the drawings against the ICIT transcription;
- the pre-registered holdout test (`copper_image_test.md`).

**Not ours:** the observation itself, and the substitutions. The preprint draft was revised to say so.
