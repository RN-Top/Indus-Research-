# Copper tablets, G407/G617 and water: scouting look (EXPLORATION, not a registered test)

*15 October 2026. Exploration split only (1,523 texts; damaged dropped, duplicates removed, unidentified signs dropped). The holdout was not read, and nothing was registered or tested. Water distances are the site-level figures already in `findspots_waterways.json`: present sea and major rivers (Natural Earth), with nothing recomputed. No p-values; this is description.*

## Question

Do the copper tablets, and texts containing closing signs G407 or G617, sit at more water-connected find-spots than other texts? This would bridge the copper result (`copper_closer_test.md`) and the water result (`findspot_gazetteer_test.md`).

## 3. Do they look more waterside than the corpus?

- **Copper tablets: yes on the surface.** 89% are within 10 km of a major river, against 53% of all texts.
- **But that is the same fact as "copper tablets are from Mohenjo-daro".** Distance to water here is measured per site, not per object. Mohenjo-daro happens to be the site closest to a river (4 km from the Indus), so anything concentrated at Mohenjo-daro looks waterside. Copper tablets are 89% Mohenjo-daro, against 51% of all texts. Nothing in this data can tell "copper goes with water" apart from "copper tablets were a Mohenjo-daro practice".
- **G407/G617 apart from copper: barely different from the corpus.** 55% at Mohenjo-daro against 51%, and 57% within 10 km of water against 53%. There are two minor coastal touches, Lothal (2) and the Bahrain find at Hajar (1), but no visible lean to water.
- **The ancient picture would not change this.** Mohenjo-daro was on the Indus then as now, roughly. Using the ancient waterways would mostly move Kalibangan (on the Hakra) and Dholavira (on the Rann) closer to water. Neither holds many of these texts.

## Is there a clean confirmatory hypothesis here?

**Not a clean one.** Any test of "copper or G407/G617 texts sit nearer water" on this data is a test of "they come from Mohenjo-daro", because water distance only varies between sites, not within them.

**Holdout support and power:**
- The holdout has only 6 copper tablets.
- It has about 20 texts containing G407/G617.
- A site-level comparison would rest on a handful of texts at a handful of sites, so it would have low power and could not separate water from site.

**What could make it testable later:**
1. **Find context within Mohenjo-daro.** Are copper tablets found in particular parts of the city, near the Great Bath, wells or drains? That would need Mackay's and Marshall's excavation records (find-spot by block and house), which are not in this corpus.
2. **Copper tablets from other sites.** These would come from CISI or Mahadevan 1977. With enough of them from sites at different distances from water, the site confound could be broken.

**Recommendation:** do not register anything from this. Keep "copper tablets near water features *inside* Mohenjo-daro" as a lead for when excavation records are available.
