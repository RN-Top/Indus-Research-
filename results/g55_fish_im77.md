# Twelve strokes come after a fish, and go with the gaur, in two transcriptions (scouting)

*26 October 2026. Inline checks. ICIT exploration split, and Mahadevan's IM77 via paragorth's unvalidated ICIT→Mahadevan bridge (G55 = M121; fish family G220…G244 = M59, M60, M65, M67, M70, M72). IM77 covers largely the same objects as ICIT, so it checks transcription and coding, not new objects. Not confirmatory.*

## The fish comes right before twelve
| | Twelve (G55 / M121) | Other numerals | Base |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICIT: fish sign right before | **30%** (10 of 33) | 0–13% each (G16 5%, G17 13%, G4 0%, G2 4%) | fish = 9.7% of tokens |
| IM77: fish sign right before | **21%** (11 of 52) | 3.3% pooled (52 of 1,598) | p 2 × 10⁻⁶ |

The fish before twelve are mostly **G240, the fish with two antennae** (6 of 10 in ICIT), plus G233, G232 and G220. Other numerals have the opposite order: they come *before* a fish (G2 25%, G32 28%, G16 24%). That is the "six + fish", "seven + fish" pattern Parpola reads as star names (*aṟu-mīn* "six stars", the Pleiades; *eḻu-mīn*, Ursa Major). **Twelve is the one numeral that follows the fish instead.**

## A reading to test
Parpola reads the fish as "star", and later Indian lunar months are named after the star (nakṣatra) where the full moon falls. Read that way, "fish + twelve" could be a calendar term: the twelve star-months, a year reckoned by the stars. That would fit a word for "twelve" that behaves like a noun rather than a count. This is a hypothesis built on Parpola's unproven reading, not a result.
