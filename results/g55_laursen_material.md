## Where G55 sits among the round "Gulf Type" seals
G55 is on 4 of the 29 inscribed Gulf Type seals in Laursen's Fig. 11: no. 3 (Mohenjo-daro), no. 7 (Qala'at al-Bahrain), no. 9 (Saar) and no. 10 (Janabiyah). The held-out-from-export Janabiyah seal N05104 is probably the same object as no. 10, transcribed differently. Both end `… G55 … G55`. Laursen 2023 (Fig. 4.5, no. 2) singles out the Janabiyah seal as an aberrant early seal with four grooves on its boss.

| Find region of Gulf Type seal | With G55 |
|---|---|
| Bahrain (Qala'at, Saar, Janabiyah, Karzakan) | **3 of 7** |
| Mesopotamia, Iran, Failaka, Near East | 0 of 14 (Fisher p 0.026, post hoc) |
| Indus Valley (round seals found there) | 1 of 5 |

For comparison, G55 is in about 2% of seal texts at home. **On the round seals of the Indus-related merchants, and above all on those found in Dilmun itself, it is many times commoner.**

## What Laursen's papers say that bears on this
- **The merchants and their animal.** After 2100 BC a "league of seafaring merchants with ties to the Indus world" brought the round seal to the Gulf. "It is always only bulls that appear on these Gulf seals with Indus script" (2023, after Vidale 2005). Vidale identifies the animal as the wild gaur, the emblem of the western traders. Our home data show G55 seals carry the gaur 4 times more often than other seals (`g55_profile_scouting.md`).
- **The language.** In Dilmun the merchants formed a diaspora and the script was "adapted to the local Semitic dialect" (2023). The locally made series of these seals is at Bahrain (Uesugi forthcoming).
- **The Dilmun formula.** The Durand stone reads "Palace of Rimum, servant of Inzak, man of Agarum". Inzak is the god of Dilmun, and Agarum is a tribe or place. Later Dilmun seals (style III) carry invocations of Inzak (2016). The early Indus-script Gulf seals have the same kind of shape: sign group + "man" sign + sign group (`data/abroad/README.md`).
- **Guilds.** Unusual boss designs (2, 3 or 4 grooves) "may have denoted affiliation with distinct merchants' guilds operating particular long-distance routes" (2023). The Janabiyah G55 seal is one of these.
- **Gujarat.** The closest parallels to the Dilmun clay tokens are "seal-stamped circular objects found at Indus sites in Gujarat" (2023). G55 goes with the man sign on the Gujarat coast (`g55_water_scouting.md`), and G55 is somewhat commoner on tags.

## Working hypothesis (not tested)
G55 marks something the Indus-related merchants of Dilmun shared, and Gujarat with them. Candidates, in the terms of the Dilmun formula:
1. **A deity or cult name**, the "servant of Inzak" slot. The obvious candidate in Dilmun is Inzak, but no source read gives Inzak, Enki or Ninhursag the number 12.
2. **A tribe, guild or house name**, the "man of Agarum" slot. A merchants' body could be "the Twelve".
3. **A calendar or office title**, the yearly "twelve".

What would decide it:
- New Gulf Type seals from Bahrain (Uesugi forthcoming; Laursen, Raun and Herslund in preparation). Does G55 recur there, and in which slot relative to the man sign?
- Any Dilmun or Old Babylonian evidence linking 12 to Inzak, a tribe or a guild. That is a question for Laursen.
