# The twelve-stroke seal used again and again at Lothal (lead)

*26 October 2026. From ICIT metadata, plus web reading of Frenez's summaries on harappa.com.*

**Fact from the corpus.** One seal reading `G705 G500 G741 G1 G55 G220 G740 G90` ("… one, twelve, fish, jar, man") is impressed on at least **seven clay tags at Lothal**: CISI L-124, L-125, L-126, L-127, L-128, L-129 and L-206 (ICIT codes TAG:L, TAG and TAG:W). So the holder of a twelve-stroke seal sealed goods repeatedly at the port. Other twelve-stroke tags: Lothal L-198 (`G55` alone), Dholavira (`G55 G740 G90`, `G55 G740`), Harappa H-2584. On pottery: Chanhu-daro C-89 `G55 G740 G790`, Kalibangan K-417 `G55 G740 G400`.

**What could tell us what that seal sealed.** Dennys Frenez studied the **70 fired clay sealings (cretulae) from the burnt warehouse at Lothal**. The impressions on them show what they closed: doors, wooden boxes, jars, cloth or leather bags, reed bundles, wooden cages. His figure captions cite Joshi & Parpola (1987) catalogue numbers, the same L-numbers as ICIT.
- Frenez, D. (2020). "Were the cretulae (clay sealings) from the Indus port town of Lothal part of an administrative archive?" Academia.edu 127571731 (blocked from this workspace; Erin can download it).
- Frenez, D. "Indus administrative technology: new insights on Harappan stamp seals and their impressions on clay tags." Academia.edu 38269183.

## Update: H-2584 is one of Frenez's illustrated administrative sealings
The harappa.com summary of Frenez's paper (sent by Erin) says Fig. 1 shows "first specimens of clay sealings … from Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro, featuring multiple impressions of different seals and Indus Script signs scratched on the rear after removal from the sealed container/room", citing **H-2584** (Parpola et al. 2010). In ICIT, H-2584 is a twelve-stroke tag: `G55 G255 G435 G690` (ICIT N03541). So one twelve-stroke text is on a clay sealing that was stamped by more than one seal, broken off a sealed container or room, and then marked on its back. That is record-keeping, not a personal seal.
The summary also gives the warehouse facts: 70 fired sealings; impressions of doors, wooden boxes, jars, cloth or leather bags, reed bundles and wooden cages; multiple seal impressions on single sealings. Frenez reads this as "shared administrative responsibility", with the seal inscriptions "possibly recording the names or roles of officials".

## Update: Frenez 2024 read (PDF supplied by Erin)
Frenez, D. (2024). "Were the cretulae (clay sealings) from the Indus port town of Lothal part of an administrative archive? Contextual, interpretive, and comparative evidence." *South Asian Studies*.
- Rao found **93 clay sealings** at Lothal, about **70 in one cluster in the "warehouse"** on the acropolis (twelve mud-brick podia, about 17 × 12 m), the rest scattered in the lower town.
- The sealings closed rooms (peg-on-wall doors), wooden lockers and structures, wooden cages, wooden boxes, pottery jars, coarse cloth and leather bags, and passing ropes.
- Up to five different seals are impressed on one sealing. Frenez reads the cluster as a temporary **administrative archive**, as in storeroom A340 at Arslantepe, rather than (as Rao thought) seals on imported cargo.
- **Figure 9 shows L-206, a twelve-seal sealing, as the example of a sealing that closed a DOOR (peg-on-wall).**

## What the ICIT codes add
The Lothal tag sub-codes match Frenez's closure types: TAG:B = box (L-161 to L-170, including his wooden-box example L-163), TAG:W = wall or door (L-206, his door example). TAG:L (L-124 to L-128, L-138) is probably locker or leather bag. That is not confirmed, and Frenez's locker examples L-143, L-144 and L-148 are coded plain TAG.

| Seal text | Times used on Lothal sealings | Closures |
|---|---|---|
| `G817 G2 G48 G740` | 10 | all boxes (TAG:B) |
| **`G705 G500 G741 G1 G55 G220 G740 G90`** (the twelve seal) | **7** | 5 × TAG:L, 1 × TAG, **1 × door** |
| `G861 G2 G236` | 2 | |
| `G861 G2` | 2 | |
| `G740 G151` | 2 | |
| all other texts | 1 each | |

Of 55 distinct seal texts on the 73 Lothal tags in ICIT, **the twelve seal is the second most used**, and it sealed more than one kind of closure, **including a storeroom door**. The holder of "… twelve, fish, jar, man" controlled access to rooms and containers in the Lothal warehouse. That is direct physical evidence that the text names an official with storeroom authority, which is what an office called "the Twelve" would be.

**Limits.**
- Whether L-124 to L-129 are in the warehouse cluster or the lower town is not stated in the paper (it lists only figure examples).
- The TAG:L meaning is inferred.
- One seal is one official; it does not prove that G55 alone means the office.
