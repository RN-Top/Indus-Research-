# G55, the twelve-stroke sign: number, unit or name part? (scouting, exploration only)

*26 October 2026. Script `scripts/g55_scouting.py`, output `g55_scouting_output.txt`. Exploration split only; the held-out-from-export set was not read. Not a confirmatory test.*

**Why.** G55 (strokes 4/4/4) is on five or six Gulf seals, three of them right next to a man sign (`data/abroad/README.md`). If G55 is simply "12", at home it should act like the other stacked numerals and stand before a counted sign.

**Data.** 38 uses (33 in distinct texts) in exploration: Mohenjo-daro 19, Harappa 8, Lothal 7 (six copies of one text), Chanhu-daro 1, plus Bahrain, Saar and Susa.

## G55 does not behave like the other numerals

Distinct texts:

| Sign | Uses | Next sign is a content (middle) sign | Next sign is a closer | Previous sign is a numeral |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G13 | 14 | 64% | 0% | 21% |
| G16 | 21 | 71% | 10% | 57% |
| G17 | 38 | 79% | 16% | 26% |
| **G55** | **33** | **30%** | **36%** | 18% |

- The other stacked numerals almost always come right before a content sign, the thing being counted. G55 does so less than half as often. More often it comes **straight before a closing sign**, mostly the jar G740 (8 times; 4.6 expected).
- G55 often opens a text (11 of 38) or follows a sign group directly.
- **G240 G55 recurs:** 6 times against about 1 expected (for example `G235 G240 G55 G740`, `G806 G32 G220 G240 G55 G740`). G55 also sits inside the recurring unit `G255 G435`, on either side of it.
- At home, G55 is next to a man sign only once in 38 (Harappa, `G920 G55 G1 G125`). Other stacked numerals: 0 of 89.

## Home and abroad

- Lothal (one text, six copies): `G705 G500 G741 G1 G55 G220 G740 G90`. G55 is followed by the fish G220, and the text **ends in the man sign** G90.
- Saar (Bahrain): `G55 G220 G91 G1 G93 G31`. G55 is again followed by the fish, then the man sign G91.
- Bahrain: `G160 G90 G55 G60 G190`. Janabiyah: `… G829 G55 G125 G90 G55` and `G71 G31 G831 G55 G121 G99 G55`.

So "G55 + fish + man sign" appears both at Lothal, a port, and at Saar. In the Gulf, G55 next to a man sign is common (3 of 6). At home it is rare (1 of 38).

## Reading

- **G55 is probably not an ordinary count.** It rarely stands before a counted thing and often ends a group before the closer. It acts more like a word or title than "12 of something". That fits a fixed unit (a measure or a calendar or ritual number, "the twelve") or part of a name.
- **The Gulf use is different.** Abroad, G55 clusters with man signs, which fits the Gulf name formula. At home it mostly does not. Either Gulf writers used G55 in names more, or the Gulf texts are a different genre (personal seals) from most home texts.
- The pattern "G55 G220 … man sign" at Lothal and Saar is a thin but concrete link between a Gujarat port and Bahrain.
