# G55 and water: sea or river, and a Gujarat–Gulf pattern (scouting, descriptive)

*26 October 2026. Script `scripts/g55_water_scouting.py`, output `g55_water_scouting_output.txt`. Uses already-seen material, so this is description, not a test.*

**Question (Erin).** If twelve is a moon or tide count, the number of the year's months, is G55 commoner where people lived by the water, judged by the waterways of Harappan times rather than today's?

**Classes.** These were fixed before counting, using `data/ancient_waterways.json`:
- *Sea:* coast, tidal estuary, the Rann of Kutch (then open water), Gulf islands and coasts, and Ur.
- *River:* a major river or the then-flowing Ghaggar-Hakra.
- *Dry:* hills or oases.

| Setting | Distinct texts | With G55 | Per 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sea | 389 | 9 | 23 |
| River | 2,877 | 42 | 15 |
| Dry | 44 | 0 | 0 |

The sea vs river difference (p 0.15) comes **entirely from the Gulf**. The Indian coast alone has the same rate as the river cities (6/372 vs 42/2,877, p 0.48). **So G55 is not a sea word as such.**

## What did show up: G55 with a man sign, on the Gujarat coast and in the Gulf

| Region | G55 texts that also have a man sign (G90, G91, G125) | All texts with a man sign |
|---|---|---|
| Gujarat coast (Lothal, Dholavira) | **2 of 6** | 6% |
| Gulf (Bahrain, Saar, Janabiyah) | **2 of 3** (more in Laursen 2010: Laursen 7, 10) | 50% |
| Indus and Ghaggar river cities | 1 of 41 | 6% |

Gujarat vs rivers: 2/6 vs 1/41, one-sided Fisher p ≈ 0.04. This was noticed after looking, so it is not evidence yet.

The texts:
- Dholavira `G55 G740 G90`
- Lothal `G705 G500 G741 G1 G55 G220 G740 G90`
- Bahrain `G160 G90 G55 G60 G190`
- Saar `G55 G220 G91 G1 G93 G31`
- Janabiyah (Laursen 10) `… G829 G55 G125 G90 G55`

On the Gujarat coast, the man sign comes at the end, after G55 (and G740). In the Gulf it comes right next to G55.

**Reading.** In the river cities, G55 is a general word: it is common there and almost never goes with a man sign. On the Gujarat coast and in the Gulf, it keeps company with man signs. Gujarat was the Indus region facing the Gulf trade. A round Gulf-type seal was found at Lothal, and the Gulf script is thought to derive from the Indus script. So a Gujarat formula, "G55 … man", could be the source of the Gulf usage. Six and three texts are far too few to say so.
