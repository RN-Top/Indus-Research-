# Twelve strokes: "the year" or "the Twelve"? (exploratory comparison)

*26 October 2026. Script `scripts/g55_year_vs_title.py`; outputs `g55_year_vs_title_output.txt` and `g55_year_vs_title_signs.csv`. Erin's question: does G55 mean the year (twelve moons) or a body, office or title ("the Twelve")? All data already seen, so this is a comparison, not a test.*

**Method.** Each reading predicts different company for the sign. G55 is ranked on each measure against 86 ordinary signs of similar frequency (15–120 uses, not numerals or man signs). The 50th percentile means "ordinary".

| Reading | Measure | G55 | Median sign | G55 percentile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year | Counted: a numeral right before it ("2 years") | 15% | 12% | 62 |
| Year | On tags or tablets (records) rather than seals | 17% | 20% | 29 |
| Year | Shares a text with a numeral + fish pair (Parpola's star names) | 12% | 8% | 74 |
| Title | Shares a text with a man sign | 12% | 5% | 86 |
| Title | On seals with the gaur or the W-marked unicorn | 71% | 36% | 93 |
| Title | Jar G740 right after it | 28% | 6% | 82 |

**Year: 55th percentile on average, so G55 is ordinary on these measures. Title: 87th percentile.**

**Bias, and why the result still says something.**
- The three title measures were chosen *after* seeing G55 behave that way (the gaur, the man sign and the jar came from the earlier scouting). Their high ranks are partly built in.
- The three year measures were new, written down before computing, and **G55 came out ordinary on them**. It is not counted more than other nouns, not commoner on record-keeping tags and tablets, and only mildly linked to "number + fish" pairs.

So the fair reading is: **the year predictions failed, and the title pattern stands as previously seen.** "The Twelve" as a title, office or body (perhaps named after the year) fits the data better than "year" as a plain noun.

**What would confirm it:** the sealed predictions P11 (man sign, Gujarat and Gulf) and P12 (gaur) on new finds. A new prediction for the year reading is not worth sealing: its own measures already came out ordinary.
