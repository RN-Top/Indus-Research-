# G700: the unit sign of the long-stroke numbers (Wells's "volumetric" sign) (scouting, descriptive)

*31 Oct 2026. Merged ICIT corpus. Prompted by Wells's volumetric proposal (Wells 1998 thesis §2.3 and Fig. 2.3, p. 39, supplied by Erin; Wells 2015, ch. 4, not read). Builds on the confirmed result `H-2026-10-14-97716181` (short and long strokes are followed by different signs) and `numbers_vs_weights.md`.*

**Identification.**
- Wells's Fig. 2.3 "Possible Volumetric System" shows Harappa tablets H-799 to H-802. In the corpus their reverses read `G33 G700`, `G33 G700`, `G36 G700` and `G32 G700`.
- So **Wells's "V" measure sign is G700**, and his "strokes" are the **long-stroke** numerals G31–G36.
- Wells (1998, p. 34): "Several vessels from Harappa have inscriptions with the [V] and one to four signs inscribed on their rims". He reads a capacity unit from three pots whose stroke counts (3, 6, 7) match their capacity ratios. Press reports put this at about 9–10 L per stroke.

**Corpus-wide test (new).**

| | Followed by G700 |
|---|---|
| Long strokes (G31–G36), all objects | **367 of 1,101 (33%)** |
| Short strokes (G1–G7), all objects | **12 of 1,117 (1%)** |
| Long strokes, excluding Harappa tablets | 14 of 623 (2.2%) |
| Short strokes, excluding Harappa tablets | 6 of 957 (0.6%) |

- Fisher odds ratio 46, p ≈ 10⁻¹⁰⁸ for all objects.
- Even without Harappa tablets, G700 still goes with long strokes about 4× more often.
- **Unit-first order also occurs:** the numerals right after G700 are long strokes 77 times and short strokes 3 times.
- Long strokes are G700's partner in both orders, "N + G700" and "G700 + N".
- Short strokes instead precede item signs: G240, G390, G803, G220 and G156 (the counted-item signs).

**On pottery.**
- G700 occurs with long strokes on pots: H-371 `G36 G700` (Period 3C-2), H-372 `G33 G700`, M-1374 `G33 G700`, M-1588 `G700 G31`, H-2272 `G700 G33`.
- G700 also occurs on **Period 2** (pre-urban) pots at Harappa: `G700 G31` (H-1550), `G700`, and `G700 G700`.
- H-371 (6) and H-372 (3) may be among Wells's measured pots. This needs checking against Wells 2015, ch. 4.

**Tablets.** 326 Harappa tablets read just `G32/G33/G34 + G700`, i.e. 2, 3 or 4 units, and almost never any other value: a fixed set of **denominations**.

**Reading.**
- The script has **two number systems**:
  - **short strokes count items** (fish, G390, etc.);
  - **long strokes go with a unit sign, G700**, which is what a measure system looks like.
- Wells's capacity measurements give G700 a candidate physical meaning: a **unit of volume** of about 9–10 L.
- G700 + long strokes appear on pots from the pre-urban Period 2 onward, and as 2/3/4-unit tablets in the city. That is the shape of a measuring practice that began on containers and was later formalised as tokens.
- **This is the strongest "ground" in the project:** a sign tied to a number system, to containers, and (via Wells) to measured capacities. It is a meaning (unit), not a sound.

**Needed to make it a test, not a hint.**
1. Wells 2015, ch. 4: the pot inventory numbers, measured capacities, and how capacity was measured.
2. Pre-register: on new pottery, G700 co-occurs with long strokes, not short strokes, beyond chance. This could go with P15.
3. If the pots can be identified in CISI, check that stroke value and capacity are proportional across **more than three** pots.

**Prior work.**
- Wells (1998; 2015) proposed G700 = capacity measure.
- The short/long contrast was confirmed in this project (`H-2026-10-14-97716181`).
- The quantitative exclusivity of G700 to long strokes, in both orders, and the Period 2 pottery occurrences are not found stated in Wells 1998. Wells 2015 is unread, so novelty is unconfirmed.
