# Groups of twelve in cultures connected to the Indus (reading notes)

*26 October 2026. Prompted by Erin: if G55 is "the Twelve" (an office or group), what groups of twelve existed among the Indus's neighbours? Secondary sources only (Wikipedia, ORACC). Nothing here is an Indus test.*

| Group | Culture and date | What the twelve are | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Twelve Ādityas | Vedic India. **6–8 in the Rigveda; 12 from the Brāhmaṇas** (c. 800–600 BC) and standard in the Purāṇas | Solar gods, **one for each month**; they include Mitra (contracts, alliances) and Varuṇa (waters, oaths) | Ties a "group of twelve" to the year of twelve months, but much later than the Indus seals |
| Dvādaśāha | Vedic (Aitareya and Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇas) | A 12-day soma rite, performed as a *sattra* (a joint session of many priests) | A corporate ritual body keyed to twelve |
| Twelve gods of the underworld | Hittite and Hurrian (Late Bronze Age, Yazılıkaya c. 13th century BC) | Twelve gods depicted in procession | A group of twelve deities in the Bronze Age Near East, late and far west |
| Twelve district governors | Israel, 1 Kings 4:7 (text later than c. 1000 BC) | "Each one had to provide supplies for one month in the year" | **Model of a body of twelve rotating by month**, combining group and year |
| Enki/Ea | Sumer and Babylonia; Dilmun myth (*Enki and Ninhursag*) | Water god and patron of Dilmun's sweet water; in the late divine-number system Ea = 40; seven sages; eight healing gods | Dilmun's mythic water god, but no tie to twelve found |
| Inzak | Dilmun (inscriptions c. 1800 BC onward) | Chief god of Dilmun, son of Enki | No tie to twelve found |

**Not found:** any group of twelve, or god of twelve, attested in Mesopotamia, Elam or Dilmun around 2100–1900 BC, the time of the Gulf Type seals. Wikipedia's article on the Ur III *bala* tax system does not describe a monthly rotation of provinces, so it is not claimed here.

**The model worth testing:** a body of twelve whose members each serve one month, so that "the Twelve" means both a group and a year. That would explain why G55 behaves as a title (group) and is still a number of the year. Predictions to think through before any test:
- the man sign as a member;
- high-rank seals;
- a limited set of "member" phrases attached to the G55 formula at one site.
