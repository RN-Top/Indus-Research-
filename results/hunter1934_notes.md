# Hunter 1934: reading notes

*20 October 2026. G. R. Hunter (1934), *The Script of Harappa and Mohenjodaro and its Connection with Other Scripts* (London: Kegan Paul). Copy from the University of Florida Digital Collections (https://ufdc.ufl.edu/AA00013642/00001), supplied by Erin; not copied into this repository. Page numbers are the book's printed numbers. The OCR loses every sign drawing, so passages were read from page images.*

## What Hunter already did

- **Method (p. 3):** he assigns functions to signs "from the regularity of their occurrence in particular positions and contexts". That is this project's method, 90 years earlier, without statistics or a held-out test.
- **Functions he proposed (p. 3):** numerals; an ordinal suffix; a word for "servant" and its determinative; an ablative suffix; a dative suffix; a word for "slave"; a word for "son". His "servant" reading parallels this project's comparison of the closer G740 with Mesopotamian *arad₂-zu* ("your servant"); cite Hunter as prior for the idea.
- **Object types (p. 3):** "the coins [copper tablets] bear the same names as the seals, votive tablets, and receipts, but … without the dedicatory preface often found on the seals and votive tablets". An early statement that formula differs by object type (compare `genre_test.md`, `copper_image_test.md`).
- **Added strokes (pp. 1–2 of the summary, and the table analyses):** "one of the cardinal features of the script is a system of modifying basic signs by internal and external strokes", compared with Sumerian *gunû* signs. He judges many such strokes to be "without effect on sense or sound", and others to change the vowel of a syllable.
- **The jar (p. 55–56):** the jar sign "at any rate [when] final … is an affix", from "its normal position at the end of the text" and the well-defined groups before it.
- **A single stroke (p. 90):** considered and rejected as a mark of punctuation ending the text, because only three of over 750 texts end with it.
- **Finals after the jar (p. 89, Table XXVII):** a group of variant signs identified "partly [by] their position as finals, but particularly as finals after the jar".
