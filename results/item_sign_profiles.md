# What gets counted, and what is a fixed number formula? (scouting, 1 Nov 2026)

Script `scripts/item_sign_profiles.py`, output `item_sign_profiles_output.txt`. Merged ICIT corpus. "Counted" means the sign comes right after a short-stroke or stacked numeral.

**Two kinds of "number + sign".**

| Kind | Signs | Values taken | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real counts** | **G390** | 2–9, mostly 3–7 | Things counted in varying numbers |
| | **G900** | 3–5 | Things counted in varying numbers |
| | **G220** (fish) | 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 | Things counted in varying numbers |
| | **G840** | 2, 3, 6 | Things counted in varying numbers |
| **Fixed formulas** | G240, G803, G233, G235, G798 | almost always **2** (G240 84 of 91; G803 55 of 63) | "2 + X" is a set phrase, not a count. It fits the project's reading of G2 as a formula marker, not "two". |
| | G585 | always **7** (30 of 30) | "7 + G585" is a fixed name or title, compare "the Seven" |
| | G861 | always **3** (29 of 30, on tablets) | Fixed phrase |
| | G156 | mostly 3 (36 of 42) | Fixed phrase |

**Reading.**
- Only a few signs are truly counted things: G390, G900, the fish G220, and G840.
- Most "number + sign" pairs are frozen phrases. The number belongs to the phrase, like "the Twelve" (G55) or "the Seven" (G585), not to a quantity.
- This splits the Count slot of `structure_meaning_map.md` in two: **real quantities** and **number-names**.
- It also explains why the copper-tablet and seal texts can carry numerals without being accounts.

**Next.** Ask what the four real counted signs denote. Use their objects (G390 is also on 8 pots), their seal animals (G390 seals are mostly unicorn) and the size of the counts. Small counts (2–9) suggest large or valuable units, such as animals, bales or people, not beads or grain.
