**A flaw in the frozen exclusion rule, found after the run.** The rule dropped a seal only if it shared a sign pair, in the same order, with an abroad text already analysed. Three kept seals (12 Failaka, 14 Susa, 17 Ur) share two or more signs with such a text from the same site and are probably the same objects read in the other direction. **Without them: 6 seals, 0.84 vs 0.49, +0.35, p 0.015.** The result holds either way, but the honest count of new objects is 6.

**Descriptive, after the run.**
- The human figure G90 opens 4 of the 9 seals (12, 14, 17, 25), and the twin men G91 opens 2 (7, 24). Laursen (2010: 116–118) proposed these "man" signs mark personal names.
- G55, twelve strokes in three rows, occurs on seals 7 and 10 (Bahrain). It is a rare sign at home.

**Reading.** A second, independently transcribed set of Gulf seals shows the same thing as the corpus abroad texts: Indus signs combined in ways the home script does not use, very often opened by a human-figure sign. Both point to the script being used abroad for other words, most likely names.
