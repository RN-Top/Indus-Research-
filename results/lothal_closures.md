**Reading.** One text sealed only boxes; another sealed every kind of container in the warehouse. The second fits a text naming an office or authority, not a commodity (Mukhopadhyay 2023); the first could be a single consignment of boxes. Two repeated texts are not enough to generalize.

**Update 28 Oct 2026: Frenez 2024 checked.** This paper (*South Asian Studies* 40:2, "Were the Cretulae ... Part of an Administrative Archive?") gives context, not sign texts. Its points:
- There are 93 sealings, about 70 in a single cluster that fell from an upper floor when the warehouse burned. It reads as a stored archive, comparable to Arslantepe A340.
- Two Lothal seals (L-6, L-37) stamped three sealings. Up to five different seals appear, some together on one sealing, which suggests shared responsibility among officials.
- What was sealed includes doors, lockers, cages, boxes, jars, and sacks or bags.

It adds no new texts, so the ~66 missing texts stay missing. The likely source is Parpola (2007), "Seal Impressions on the Clay Tags from Lothal: A Re-Analysis" (Osada ed., *Linguistics, Archaeology and the Human Past*, Occasional Paper 2, pp. 1–12).
