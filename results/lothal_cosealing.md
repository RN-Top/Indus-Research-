## Comparison with every other seal at Lothal
71 sealings carry 61 distinct seal texts:

| Seal | Sealings | Different seals stamped with it | Closure types |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The twelve seal** | **11** | **5** | **7** (straw, box, sack, pot, structures, door, unclear) |
| Box seal (`G861/G817 G2 G48 G740`) | 10 | 0 | 1 (wooden boxes only) |
| Any other seal | ≤ 3 | ≤ 2 | ≤ 2 |

5 of the twelve seal's 11 sealings are co-sealed, against 7 of the other 60 sealings (Fisher p ≈ 0.016, one-sided, after looking).

## Reading
At Lothal's warehouse the holder of the twelve seal:
- sealed the most closures;
- sealed the widest range of things (straw packages, boxes, sacks, pots, cages, storeroom structures, a door);
- **is the only seal that regularly countersigned with other officials**, five different ones, one of whom held a seal ending in a man sign.

The box seal, by contrast, worked alone on one kind of container. That is the profile of a supervising office, the person whose seal accompanies others' when storerooms are opened. It fits Frenez's "shared administrative responsibility" and the "title, office" reading of twelve (`g55_group_models.md`). Paragorth S226 recorded the twelve seal's closure range. The co-sealing role and the 11-sealing count are new here.

**Limits.**
- One site and one seal.
- The second seal on L-206 is unread.
- Closure types are Frenez & Tosi's.
- Descriptive, after looking.
- The full per-sealing seal table (Frenez, "Mirrored Signs", Table 1; Parpola 1986, World Archaeology 17(3), Fig. 1) would confirm the co-impressions.

**Corrections to the text above.**
- The twelve seal is on **15** sealings by Frenez's count, which makes it **the most used seal at Lothal**, ahead of the elephant box seal (12).
- It is not the only countersigning seal. Unicorns 2, 5 and 6 also co-seal, and Unicorn 6 never seals alone.

**What stands.**
- The twelve seal is the **principal** seal: the most sealings, the widest range of closures, and a partner of three of the other four co-sealing seals.
- The elephant seal (whose text is also a Mohenjo-daro seal) never co-seals.
- L-189 carries four recurring seals at once: Unicorn 1, 2, 5 and 6.
- Unicorn 6 co-seals with Unicorn 1 on L-193, and the corpus reads L-193's second impression as `G70 G125`, ending in a man sign. If that is Unicorn 6, the pure countersigner's seal names a person.

Frenez's own reading: "no clear patterns emerged … Perhaps, they indicated the sharing of ownership or, most probably, of storage space or administrative duties on the control of specific containers and stored goods" (2020: 27).
