# Is Mahadevan's Phu Khao Thong match rarer than chance? (no holdout used)

*22 October 2026. Script: `scripts/mahadevan_match_null.py`, run on Mahadevan's own corpus (IM77, github.com/paragorth/Indus- `data/im77`, not copied here). Output: `mahadevan_match_null_output.txt`. Follows "Testable next steps" 1 in `agent_survivals_2026-10-22.md`.*

**The claim.** A sherd from Phu Khao Thong, Thailand (with Tamil-Brahmi of about the 2nd century CE) carries two signs: an "N"-like sign (≈ Mahadevan nos. 47/48) followed by a diamond (≈ nos. 261/373). Mahadevan said the same pair occurs in Harappa texts 4589 and 5265.

**1. Checking the claim in IM77.**
- **5265: confirmed.** It reads M373 M48 M373, so the pair is there in both orders.
- **4589: not confirmed.** It reads M48 M47 M379 M245 M245 / M307 M267 / M87 M328. It has both "N" signs but no diamond.
- The pair also occurs in 5267, 5339 (M48→M373) and 3247, 5221 (M373→M48). Every hit uses M48 and M373, the two commonest of the four candidates (168 and 61 uses).

**2. How likely is a match by chance?** A random 2-sign graffito is drawn from the Indus inventory, with two candidate Indus signs allowed per graffito sign, as in the claim. Does any of the candidate pairs occur somewhere in the corpus?

| How the random signs are drawn | Fixed order | Either order |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted by how often each sign is used | **72%** | **87%** |
| Same frequency as the four claimed candidates (±50%) | **27%** | **45%** |
| Every sign type equally likely (unrealistic: simple shapes are common signs) | 6% | 10% |

Only 1.6% of all possible ordered pairs are attested, but the common signs are the ones simple marks resemble, and they pair widely.

**Verdict: not rarer than chance.** With signs as common as the ones claimed, a match turns up somewhere in the corpus about one time in four (fixed order) to one in two (either order), and the sherd's reading direction is not known. One of the two texts Mahadevan cited does not contain the pair in his own corpus as exported. The match neither supports nor rules out a link; it is the kind of coincidence the corpus produces routinely.

**Limits.** One claim tested. The Sulur dish and the paired-arrow pots could not be tested because their sign numbers were not retrievable. The graffito-to-Indus sign identifications are Mahadevan's; a different identification would change the candidates. IM77 as exported from indusscript.in may differ from the printed 1977 concordance for text 4589.
