# Does the mark or the base decide where a derived sign goes? (scouting, exploration only)

*22 October 2026. Script: `scripts/mark_vs_base.py`. 48 derived signs with 5+ uses and exactly one mark class (inner 32, top 10, outer 6), on 22 root bases. Position profile = share first, share last, mean relative position; Euclidean distance between signs. Null: mark-class labels permuted among the signs. Not a confirmatory test.*

| Pairs of derived signs | Mean profile distance | Pairs |
|---|---|---|
| Same mark class, different base | 0.556 | 524 |
| Same base, different mark class | 0.418 | 23 |

Difference −0.14 (shuffled −0.11; p 0.66 for mark-sharing pairs being closer). **Not supported:** signs that share a kind of mark do not end up in the same place across different bases; signs on the same base stay closer to each other.

**Reading.** Marks act as *relative* operators, not absolute slot labels: a mark on top moves a sign earlier than its own base, and corner strokes move it later than its own base (both confirmed), but where the sign lands still depends on the base it started from. This qualifies `rare_compounds.md`: a derived sign's position is not predicted by its base's position alone, nor by its mark alone; it is the base shifted by the mark.

**Caveats.** Dominated by inner marks, which have no positional effect; only 16 top or outer signs reach 5 uses.
