# Marks inside signs: does an added mark move a sign to the end? (EXPLORATION)

*20 October 2026. Exploration split only. Nothing registered.*

**The idea.** We have treated every G-number as a separate sign. Many are a **base sign plus a small added mark**: strokes between a jar's handles, a "roof" over a fish, four short strokes enclosing a sign. If a mark does the same thing to many different bases, it works like a grammatical affix inside the script.
- Wells (1998) calls this "field shifting": markings change a sign's field.
- He describes it; he does not test it across bases.

## 1. Marks shift position

| Base | Plain sign | With a mark |
|---|---|---|
| Jar | G740 ends texts (68% final) | G741, G742, G745 (strokes added) move **forward**: mean position 0.24–0.37, almost never final |
| Fish | G220: middle | G235 (roof) moves **to the front**, opening texts 37% of the time. G226 (four corner strokes) moves **to the end**, 47% final. |

## 2. One mark, same effect on six different bases: the enclosure

"Enclosure" means four short strokes around the sign, or brackets.

| Base (plain) | Plain: final or just before the closer | Enclosed variant | Enclosed: final or just before the closer | Enclosed: text-final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G220 fish | 30% (n = 180) | G226 | 63% (n = 19) | 47% |
| G231 fish with stroke | low, 2% final (n = 43) | G232 | 57% (n = 7) | 43% |
| G233 fish with bar | low, 3% final (n = 102) | G234 | 75% (n = 4) | 50% |
| G235 fish with roof | 0% final (n = 126) | G236 | 50% (n = 12) | 42% |
| G240 fish with antennae | 2% final (n = 153) | G241 | 100% (n = 7) | 100% |
| G803 leaf oval | 2% final (n = 66) | G804 | 80% (n = 5) | 40% |
| G220 fish, in brackets | — | G222 | 89% (n = 9) | 0%: just before the closer |

**In every family, the enclosed variant sits at the end of the text, or just before the closer, far more often than its plain base.** The direction is the same in 6 of 6 base families.

**Reading:**
- The enclosure mark behaves like a **final-position marker**, an ending added to a sign.
- That is exactly what a grammatical suffix would do, or a "closing" category mark.
- It is a regularity *inside* signs, and it is invisible when every variant is treated as unrelated.

**Caveats:**
- The counts for each variant are small: 54 enclosed tokens in all, plus G222.
- The mark identity was read from glyph shapes (Fuls sign list, Table 2.6). A second reader should check it.
- Part of the effect could come from transcription choices. For example, a sign at the end of a damaged edge might be drawn with strokes.

**Possible registration:**
- Claim: on the holdout, enclosed variants (a frozen list) sit in the end region more often than their plain bases.
- Expected holdout support: about 15–17 enclosed tokens. That is borderline.
- Not registered yet.
