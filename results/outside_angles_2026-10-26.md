# Outside angles on the twelve-stroke sign (scouting notes)

*26 October 2026, evening. Erin asked for thinking outside the box: scripts are deciphered from outside (bilinguals, known names, neighbours' scripts), not only from internal statistics. Exploration data only where data was used.*

## 1. Linear Elamite (a neighbour's script, deciphered by Desset et al. 2022)
- About 51 texts are known: Susa (18), silver vessels from the highlands (24), and 8 short "Elamo-Bactrian" inscriptions from the east.
- No known Linear Elamite text mentions Meluhha. None sits on an object with Indus signs.
- One seal in Laursen 2010 (no. 28, western Iranian plateau) is in Linear Elamite, not Indus.
- **Status:** no bilingual now. Watch the eastern (Central Asian) finds, where Indus seals also occur (Gonur).

## 2. Gulf seals and Semitic names (idea, not run)
Laursen (2023) thinks the Gulf seals write a Semitic language. Amorite and Akkadian names of the period have strict two-part shapes, with a divine element such as "-El" that repeats. On the Janabiyah seal (ICIT N05104), twelve ends both sign groups: `G71 G31 G831 G55 | G121 G99 | G55`. That is the pattern a repeating name element would make, like "Yagli-El … -El". With about 10 Bahrain seals this cannot be tested yet. It needs the unpublished Bahrain material (Uesugi; Laursen et al.).

## 3. Copper picture-names next to twelve
The copper tablets give phrases tied to one picture (`copper_anchors.md`): `G706 G33` = elephant, `G235 G705 G33` = hare, `G798 G240` = tiger (with the antenna fish), `G3 G423` = gaur, `G32 G220` = composite animal.
- Twelve-stroke texts that contain such a phrase: **8 of 33 (24%)**. Texts of similar length: 14.6%. One-sided Fisher p 0.11, so this is not significant.
- Examples:
  - `G235 G706 G33 G55 G740` (elephant phrase + twelve + jar);
  - `G55 G745 G104 G263 G3 G423` (twelve with the **gaur** phrase);
  - `G806 G32 G220 G240 G55 G740` (composite-animal phrase + antenna fish + twelve + jar).
- **Reading (speculative):** "[figure's name] + twelve + jar" could be a title tied to a figure or emblem. It is a weak trend only.

## 4. What the seals sealed (idea, not run)
The backs of clay sealings show what they closed (jar, bag, door). Frenez and others have recorded this for Indus sealings. Matching sealing texts to what they closed would tell what the words were about. This needs those published sealing-back data. They are not in the repo yet.
