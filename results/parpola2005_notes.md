# Parpola (2005), "Study of the Indus Script": what it gives us

*Reading notes, 20 October 2026. Erin supplied the PDF; it is not copied here. Page numbers are the article's.*

## Points that bear directly on our work

1. **Function (p. 51).**
   - Tag impressions show that seals controlled economic administration and trade.
   - Seal texts "chiefly contain proper names of persons with or without their occupational or official titles and descent", as on Mesopotamian seals.
   - This is the hypothesis our confirmed rare-first-sign result supports from the inside: name-like slots at the edges, and fixed closers.
2. **Foreign seals (p. 47).**
   - Round (Gulf) and cylinder (Mesopotamian-type) seals use common Indus signs in sequences "completely dissimilar" from native texts.
   - Parpola's reading: Harappan agents abroad were bilingual, took local names, and wrote them in Indus signs.
   - **Our overseas-sign observations (G91, G924) are therefore anticipated in substance.**
3. **Copper tablets are "semi-bilinguals" (p. 58).**
   - Copper tablets from Mohenjo-daro come in sets of identical tablets.
   - The inscription on one face goes with a fixed image on the other.
   - In some sets, a single sign stands where another set has the animal or human figure. Parpola reads that sign as the name of what is depicted.
   - Wells (1998) used the same tablets: the hare set.
4. **Titles as sign pairs (p. 60).**
   - "Eye" + "eye" as a title ("overseer").
   - "Water" + "eye" as an irrigation official.
   - These are rebus readings, which cannot be tested. The **position** of such pairs (title slot) can be tested.
5. **The prior-work list for administration:** Frenez and Tosi on the Lothal sealings (cited as in press).

## The lead this opens: copper tablets as a meaning key

In our exploration split, copper tablets fall into repeated text–image sets. **The image is known independently of the text.** That makes it a route to **meaning without sound values**.

| Text (reading order) | Image on the tablet |
|---|---|
| G235 G705 G33 G845 G407 G321 G407 | **hare**, in all 8 copies |
| G3 G421 G176 G100 G740 G790 | **goat**, in all 3 |
| G32 G71 G407 | **gaur** (wild bull), 2 |
| G415 G220 G845 G407 | **loop** motif, 3 |
| G503 G615 G752 G740 | "Othr", 25. The motif label is too coarse to use. |

**Questions that could be tested:**
- Is the text-to-image mapping more fixed than chance?
- Which signs differ between sets with different images?
  - Shared signs are the formula, for example G845 + G407, the copper closer we have already confirmed.
  - Differing signs are candidates for the name of the depicted thing.

**What it needs:** better motif labels than "Othr" and "unknown".
- CISI volume 1 has photographs of the Mohenjo-daro copper tablets.
- Fuls's corpus records "Symbol/Cult" for each object.
