# Two small checks from the Rakhigarhi and pottery work (30 Oct 2026, descriptive)

**1. The Rakhigarhi bone piece "I N I X I X X"** (Nath 2014, p. 219, described in words only).
- It contains an alternating A-B-A-B run (I X I X).
- **No text among the 5,369 in the merged ICIT corpus has an A-B-A-B run.**
- So either the excavator's letter description simplifies the signs, or the piece is not ordinary Indus writing: perhaps a tally, a decoration, or a marking system of its own.
- It needs a drawing before it can be used.

**2. Potters' stamps at Harappa.**
- Stamped pottery (`POT:T:s`, made before firing) is dominated by a single sign stamped alone: **G440 on 12 Harappa pots, every dated one Period 3C**, with square stamps.
- Also G416 alone (4, rectangular stamps, 3C), G13 alone (4, 3C), and pairs G540 G540 and G33 G220 (rectangular, 3C).
- These match Kenoyer's stamped pointed-base goblets of Period 3C (`agent_function_anchors_2026-10-22.md` 2a).
- G440 also occurs on 32 seals and 11 tablets, so the goblet stamp uses an ordinary script sign, not a potter's private mark.
- The stamped vocabulary differs from scratched graffiti (`pottery_neighbours_scouting.md`). A single official or production sign stamped on mass-produced cups is a different practice from owners scratching marks.
