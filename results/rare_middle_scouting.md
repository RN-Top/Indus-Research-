# Rare signs in the middle of inscriptions: built from common parts? (scouting, exploration only)

*23 October 2026. Script: `scripts/rare_middle_scouting.py`; output `rare_middle_scouting_output.txt`. Middle as in H-2026-10-16-a6cd844c. Rare = 5 or fewer uses in exploration. Built = the blind annotation gives a base (marked variant or ligature). Not a confirmatory test.*

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Rare sign types (≤5 uses), annotated | 336, of which **191 (57%) are built on a common base** |
| Share of rare tokens in the middle: built vs plain rare signs | 50.8% vs 51.3% (p 0.54): **no difference** |
| Middle tokens that are built signs: rare vs common | 56% vs 52% |
| Bases of the 117 built rare signs used in the middle | mostly middle signs (94), some closers (18); commonest base the human figure G90 (12 compounds), then G200, G220 fish, G465, G700 jar |
| **Does a rare built sign keep its own base's neighbours?** (102 types, 204 tokens) | **78.9% vs 66.6%** with a random other base (p 0.002) |

**Reading.**
- Rare signs are not a middle-only phenomenon, and being "built" does not decide where a sign sits.
- But most rare signs are a common sign with something added, and **a rare compound keeps its base's company more than chance**. So a rare sign can be studied through its base: its base's slot, neighbours and counts are evidence about it. This is the way around the "too few examples" problem.
- Combined with the fish result (an inside mark stops the fish being counted), the picture is: a built sign stays in its base's family of contexts, but the addition changes some of what it does.

**Candidate for a held-out test:** "rare built signs share neighbours with their own base more than with other bases", on the old holdout (rare status and bases fixed from exploration).
