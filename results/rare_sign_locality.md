# Are rare signs local? (scouting, exploration only)

*22 October 2026. Script: `scripts/rare_sign_locality.py`. Rare = 2–6 uses in the exploration split (duplicates removed), used in 2+ different texts: 190 signs. Statistic: share of rare signs whose uses all come from one place. Null: place labels permuted across texts (2,000 times). Not a confirmatory test.*

**Idea.** If rare signs belong to names of people, families or places, their few uses should cluster where those people lived, more than ordinary vocabulary does.

| Scale | Rare signs used in one place only | Shuffled | Ratio | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Site | 26.3% | 21.6% | 1.22 | 0.05 |
| Site, shuffled within object type | 26.3% | 25.1% | 1.05 | 0.37 |
| Excavation area, Mohenjo-daro (88 signs) | 13.6% | 11.7% | 1.17 | 0.32 |
| Excavation area, Harappa (32 signs) | 3.1% | 4.6% | 0.68 | 0.79 |

**Verdict: rare signs are not local.** A weak site clustering disappears once object type is held fixed (rare signs on, say, copper tablets are mostly at Mohenjo-daro because the copper tablets are). Within cities there is no neighbourhood clustering at all.

**Reading.** The rare signs spread across cities and neighbourhoods like the rest of the script. That does not fit personal or family names tied to one place. With the earlier result that texts without a heading start with a rare sign (`H-2026-10-16-51b7445b`), the rare first sign looks like a rare *category* or title used across the civilization, not a local person's name. This fits Mukhopadhyay's (2019, 2023) argument that the inscriptions contain no proper nouns, and the uniform, non-local character of seal texts found at every scale (`house_scouting.md`, `context_scouting.md`, `region_scouting.md`).

**Caveats.** "Rare" in a sample of 1,523 texts includes signs that are common in reality but under-sampled; names of officials who travelled, or seals carried away from home, would also scatter.
