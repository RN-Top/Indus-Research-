# Wells 1998 (MA thesis): what it already says, set against this project's results

*Reading notes, 19 October 2026. The thesis PDF was sent by Andreas Fuls. It is not copied into this repository; page numbers refer to the thesis. These are notes, not a test.*

**Source:** Bryan K. Wells (1998), MA thesis, *An Introduction to Indus Writing*. Chapter 4, "The Structure of the Indus Inscriptions", including **Column Analysis** and **Figure 4.6** (the page Fuls cites).

**Sign numbers:** the 1998 thesis uses Wells's *own early sign numbers*, not the later G-numbers in our corpus. For example, his 1998 sign 288 is the "jar", our G740. Any number below written "sign 288" is his 1998 number. A concordance would be needed for exact matching.

## What Wells did

- **Column analysis.** He took the 327 Mohenjo-daro seal inscriptions containing his sign 288, the jar, and placed signs in columns by their relative left or right order.
- **Fields.** The columns group into **three fields (I–III) and eight sub-fields (A–H)**. Under a Proto-Dravidian Subject–Object–Verb assumption he reads them as subject, object and verb.
- **Short seals as fragments.** Seals of 5 signs or fewer are treated as fragments of longer messages, meant to be combined through multiple seal impressions on tags.

## Takeaways

1. **Cite Wells (1998), Chapter 4 and Figure 4.6,** for the field structure, the markers that close the opening cluster, and suffix columns. The project's claims should be framed as *tests* of Wells's structure, plus the object-class closers, the rare-first-sign result and the cross-script comparisons.
2. **The heading-families result is very likely anticipated** by Wells's marker analysis. Treat it as confirmation unless the exact opener-to-marker exclusivity turns out not to be in Wells 1998, 2006 or 2011.
3. **Wells's interpretive layer** (Proto-Dravidian subject–object–verb, verbal case endings) is an assumption. This project stays neutral on language.
