Water then is a sketch, not a survey. Each green line joins places that a published source says lay on that channel around 2600–1900 BCE; tap one for the source. The Hakra was monsoon-fed and ended in lakes in Cholistan. The older Sutlej bed through the Nara to the Rann was already dry. The Euphrates ran past Nippur, Uruk and Ur, with an eastern branch past Umma. The Rann of Kutch and the head of the Gulf may have been open water, but both are debated. Not drawn: The Indus's own Harappan-era course in Sindh: the river has moved, but no source found here gives a usable trace, so the map shows only today's Indus. The Ravi's older channel by Harappa and the Beas palaeochannel (Singh et al. 2017): no usable trace found.

Routes are drawn by hand. They are not surveyed. The sea route follows the coast past Ras al-Hadd and Hormuz, in the order of Sargon of Akkad's inscription naming the ships of Meluhha, Magan and Dilmun. The overland routes join modern passes and towns (Khyber, Kabul, Kunduz; Bolan, Kandahar, Herat).

Coastline and rivers are modern (Natural Earth). The Indus has moved, Lothal's dock is now inland, and the Karun, Murghab, Ravi and Kokcha are missing from this river layer. About half the find-spots abroad have approximate coordinates, and "Luristan" is a region, not a site.

Find-spots reflect where people dug. Mesopotamian cities and Bahrain's burial mounds were excavated far more than the Iranian plateau or the Makran coast. 57 inscriptions from small home sites without coordinates are not shown.

"Closer" counts use exploration texts only. Holdout texts are counted but their signs were not read. The abroad figure rests on 14 texts and was noticed after looking, so it is a lead, not a finding.
